## Arithmetic and answer checks
All recomputed independently in Python with CODATA 2018 constants (ε₀ = 8.8541878128e-12 F/m, μ₀ = 1.25663706212e-6, e = 1.602176634e-19 C, m_e = 9.1093837015e-31 kg, G = 6.6743e-11, M_sun = 1.98847e30 kg, R_sun = 6.957e8 m).
- 1/(4πε₀) = 8.988 × 10⁹. Correct.
- Quiz 1 (+2, +2, −3 nC, side 3.0 cm): pair sum 4 − 6 − 6 = −8 nC²; W = 8.988e9 × (−8e-18)/0.03 = −2.397 µJ, "about −2.40 µJ" (answer index 1). Distractor without ½ over ordered pairs: −4.79 µJ. Correct; the explanation is right.
- Charged ball (R = 10 cm, Q = 50 nC): Q²/(4πε₀) = 2.247e-5 J m; W₀ = 2.247e-4 J; W = (3/5)W₀ = 1.348e-4 J ≈ 135 µJ; inside W₀/10 = 2.25e-5 J ≈ 22.5 µJ, i.e. (1/10)/(6/10) = 1/6. Potential inside Q(3 − r²/R²)/(8πε₀R) and ½∫ρV = (3/5)W₀ checked analytically. Outside energy within r: (W₀/2)(1 − R/r), so the fraction within r outside the ball is 1/6 + (5/6)(1 − R/r) = 1 − 5R/(6r): half at r = 5R/3, 10% beyond r = 8.33R. Inside: (r/R)⁵/6. All correct.
- Plot: expr `x <= 1 ? x^5/6 : 1 - 5/(6*x)` is the fraction above, continuous at x = 1 (value 1/6, the plotted point 0.1667). Domain 0-8 does not reach 8.3R, which the caption mentions only in words. Correct.
- Sun as a uniform ball: −(3/5)GM²/R = −2.276e41 J → "−2.28 × 10⁴¹ J"; Mc² = 1.787e47 J → "1.79 × 10⁴⁷ J"; ratio 1.27e-6, "about a millionth". Halving R doubles |W|, releasing another 2.28e41 J. Correct. The remark that the real, centrally condensed Sun has a larger |W| is right.
- Classical electron: r_e = 2.818 fm; R = 0.6 r_e = 1.691 fm → "1.69 fm". e²/(4πε₀) = 2.307e-28 J m; at R = 1e-18 m, W = 0.6 × 2.307e-10 = 1.384e-10 J = 864.0 MeV; ratio to 0.511 MeV = 1691 ("some 1690"); m₀c² = 0.511 − 864.0 = −863.5 MeV ("about −863"). Correct. Small point (info): "a little larger than a proton": the proton charge radius is about 0.84 fm, so 1.69 fm is about twice it; "about twice the radius of a proton" would be more accurate.
