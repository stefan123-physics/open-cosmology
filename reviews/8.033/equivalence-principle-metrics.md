## Arithmetic and answer checks
- Formula citations: Newtonian gravity (∇²φ = 4πGρ, φ = −GM/r, F = mg = −m∇φ) is on slide 12. a = GM/r² is on slide 16. The spherical-coordinate Minkowski metric is on slide 27. The four metrics, with c = G = 1, FRW with "+" signs inside the bracket, and Schwarzschild with r_s = 2M, are on slide 30. All four citations match the slides exactly. The inline items also check: ds² = dr^t g dr (slide 25); η = diag(−1, −1, −1, 1) for (x, y, z, t) and Δτ = ∫dt/γ (slide 26); the Lorentz-invariant dτ² line (slide 29).
- The page's own derivation is correct. For a clock at rest, dτ = √(1 + 2φ) dt ≈ (1 + φ) dt, so the rate difference is Δφ = gh (gh/c² in SI units). For slow motion in a weak field, √(1 + 2φ − v²) ≈ 1 + φ − v²/2; numerically, φ = −10⁻⁴ and v = 10⁻³ give a difference of −5 × 10⁻⁹, which is second order. Making ∫(1 + φ − v²/2) dt stationary is the same as making ∫(v²/2 − φ) dt stationary, and Euler–Lagrange gives dv/dt = −∇φ. Correct.
- Everest: 9.81 × 8848 / (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 9.64 × 10⁻¹³. Times 3.156 × 10⁷ s gives 30.4 μs per year; with the quiz's 3.16 × 10⁷ s, 30.5 μs. Correct.
- Sun: 2 × 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1.989 × 10³⁰ / (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 2950 m = 2.95 km. Correct. The Sun's radius of about 700,000 km is correct (695,700 km).
- Earth: 3.986 × 10¹⁴ / (6.371 × 10⁶ × 9 × 10¹⁶) = 6.95 × 10⁻¹⁰, which rounds to 7.0 × 10⁻¹⁰. Twice that is 1.39 × 10⁻⁹, which rounds to 1.4 × 10⁻⁹. Correct.
- Pound–Rebka: 2 × 9.81 × 22.6 / c² = 4.93 × 10⁻¹⁵, so about 5 × 10⁻¹⁵, matching slide 22 (see row 5).
- Quiz 1 (answer index 1, the weak equivalence principle): correct. Quiz 2 (index 3, Eötvös, 1890): correct per slide 17. Quiz 3 (index 0, about 30 μs): correct. Quiz 4 (index 2, Schwarzschild): correct and matches slide 30.
- Flashcards: the weak and strong principles match slide 13. The Newtonian metric is correct, with its sign right. General covariance matches slide 28. r_s ≈ 3 km for the Sun is correct.
- Slide 18 reading: the Eötvös bar is at about 10⁻⁸ (arrow to about 10⁻⁹), and the lunar laser ranging band and Eöt-Wash II reach about 10⁻¹²·⁵ to 10⁻¹³ near 2000. "About 10⁻⁸ … about 10⁻¹³ by 2000" is a fair reading.
