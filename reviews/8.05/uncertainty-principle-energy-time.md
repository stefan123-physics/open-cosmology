## Arithmetic and answer checks
Recomputed by hand and in Python:
- $S_x$ in z+: $\langle S_x\rangle = 0$, $\langle S_x^2\rangle = \hbar^2/4$, $\Delta S_x = \hbar/2$. Correct.
- h/4 remark: transcript p. 19 indeed says "greater than or equal to h bar over 4"; notes (3.44) has $h/4$. The page's statement that the bound is $h/4 = \pi\hbar/2$ is correct (it is the known minimum orthogonalisation time $\pi\hbar/(2\Delta H)$), and $\hbar/4$ would be weaker than the page's own $\hbar/2$ relation, so the transcript wording is a slip. The remark is right.
- Hyperfine: $\lambda = hc/E = 1239.84\ \text{eV nm}/5.88 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{eV} = 21.09$ cm, correct (21.1). $\nu = E/h = 1421.8$ MHz from the rounded 5.88; "about 1420 MHz" is fine (notes: 1420.405751786 MHz). $11\ \text{Myr} \times \pi \times 10^7$ s $= 3.46 \times 10^{14}$ s (finding 4). $\hbar/\tau = 6.582 \times 10^{-16}/3.46 \times 10^{14} = 1.90 \times 10^{-30}$ eV (with 3.4e14: $1.94 \times 10^{-30}$), "about $2 \times 10^{-30}$", correct. $\Delta E/E = 3.2 \times 10^{-25}$ ($3.3 \times 10^{-25}$ with 3.4e14), "about $3 \times 10^{-25}$", correct. The year $\approx \pi \times 10^7$ s is accurate to 0.45%.
- Worked example (a): $\frac{1}{2i}[S_x, S_y] = \frac{\hbar}{2}S_z$, $\langle S_z\rangle = \hbar/2$, bound $\hbar^2/4$; $\Delta S_y = \hbar/2$ ($S_y$ z+ $= (i\hbar/2)$ z−, $S_y^2 = (\hbar/2)^2 I$); product $\hbar^2/4$, equality. Correct. Consistent with (2.31): $g = S_y\psi = (i\hbar/2)$ z− $= i \cdot 1 \cdot f$ with $f = S_x\psi = (\hbar/2)$ z−, so $\lambda = 1 = \Delta S_y/\Delta S_x$, as (2.32) requires.

Ratio to the 21 cm relative width: $8.2 \times 10^{-8}/3.3 \times 10^{-25} = 2.5 \times 10^{17}$, "about $10^{17}$", correct to the order of magnitude.
