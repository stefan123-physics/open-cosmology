Energy eigenstates of the oscillator are extremely quantum: in a stationary state every expectation value is constant, so the particle's position and momentum "do nothing". **Coherent states** are quantum states that move like a classical particle on a spring, and they describe the light of lasers (transcript pp. 1–2, 9). The lecture builds them from the Heisenberg operators of the last lecture (transcript pp. 1–23; notes chapter 6, section 4). States are written here as $\psi$ with labels instead of kets: $\psi_0$ is the ground state, $\psi_n$ the number states, $\tilde\psi$ the displaced ground state and $\psi_\alpha$ the general coherent state. (MIT's material for this lecture is the recorded lecture and Zwiebach's notes; this page follows them in its own words, with its own worked example.)

## Reminder: a and a† in the Heisenberg picture

Since $a$ and $a^\dagger$ are combinations of $\hat x$ and $\hat p$, they also have Heisenberg versions, and these are just phases (transcript pp. 1–2): $a_H(t) = a\,e^{-i\omega t}$ and $a^\dagger_H(t) = a^\dagger e^{i\omega t}$. Putting them into $\hat x \propto a + a^\dagger$ reproduces $\hat x_H(t) = \hat x\cos\omega t + (\hat p/m\omega)\sin\omega t$.

## The translation operator

For a real number $x_0$ define (transcript p. 3)

<Formula label="Translation operator" tex="T_{x_0} = e^{-i\hat px_0/\hbar}" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.1)" />

It is unitary, since $i\hat px_0/\hbar$ is anti-Hermitian. Two translations commute and add, $T_{x_0}T_{y_0} = T_{x_0 + y_0}$, with no Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff correction because both exponents contain only $\hat p$; and $T_{x_0}^\dagger = T_{-x_0} = T_{x_0}^{-1}$ (transcript p. 3; notes eqs. (4.2)–(4.4)). An operator acts on another operator from both sides, and then (transcript p. 4; for $\hat p$, notes p. 29)

<Formula label="Translating x and p" tex="T_{x_0}^\dagger\,\hat x\,T_{x_0} = \hat x + x_0, \qquad T_{x_0}^\dagger\,\hat p\,T_{x_0} = \hat p" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.5)" />

This is what makes the definition physical: in the state $T_{x_0}\psi$, the expectation value of $\hat x$ is $(\psi, T_{x_0}^\dagger\hat xT_{x_0}\psi) = (\psi, \hat x\psi) + x_0$. The state has been moved by $x_0$ (transcript pp. 4–5; notes eq. (4.8)). Similarly a position state at $x_1$ becomes one at $x_1 + x_0$, and a wavefunction $\psi(x)$ becomes $\psi(x - x_0)$, the same shape moved to the right (transcript p. 5; notes eqs. (4.9)–(4.13)).

## The coherent state at t = 0

Displace the ground state (transcript p. 5):

<Formula label="Coherent state" tex="\tilde\psi = T_{x_0}\,\psi_0 = e^{-i\hat px_0/\hbar}\,\psi_0" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.14)" />

It is not a position eigenstate; it is normalised because $T_{x_0}$ is unitary, and its wavefunction is the ground-state Gaussian moved to $x_0$, $\psi_0(x - x_0)$ (transcript p. 6; notes eqs. (4.15)–(4.16)). To compute an expectation value, move the translation operators onto the operator and evaluate in the ground state. So $\langle\hat x\rangle = x_0$ and $\langle\hat p\rangle = 0$: at this instant the state sits at $x_0$ without moving (transcript pp. 6–7). For the energy, only the potential term changes; the cross term contains $\langle\hat x\rangle$ in the ground state, which is zero, so (transcript p. 7)

<Formula label="Energy of the coherent state" tex="\langle H\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x_0^2" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.23)" />

a small quantum term plus the classical energy of a spring stretched to $x_0$. (The transcript calls this "1/2 of k squared ... x0 squared"; the spring energy is $\frac{1}{2}kx_0^2$ with $k = m\omega^2$.) Three more results are left as exercises: $\langle\hat x^2\rangle = x_0^2 + \hbar/2m\omega$, $\langle\hat p^2\rangle = m\hbar\omega/2$ and $\langle\hat x\hat p + \hat p\hat x\rangle = 0$ (transcript p. 8; notes eqs. (4.29)–(4.33) and (4.36)). Asked about displacing excited states, Zwiebach said this is possible but more complicated, and everything can be understood from the displaced ground state (transcript p. 8).

## Coherent time evolution

The coherent state is not an energy eigenstate, so it changes in time. A non-stationary state usually changes shape, as a free wave packet spreads. This one does not (transcript pp. 8–9). Use the Heisenberg operators, which put all the time dependence on the operator (transcript pp. 9–10):

<Formula label="Mean position and momentum" tex="\langle\hat x\rangle(t) = x_0\cos\omega t, \qquad \langle\hat p\rangle(t) = -m\omega x_0\sin\omega t = m\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat x\rangle" cite="reading2" at="eqs. (4.26)–(4.28)" />

exactly the classical motion. For the width, square $\hat x_H(t)$: the term with $\hat x\hat p + \hat p\hat x$ vanishes, the $x_0^2\cos^2\omega t$ cancels against the square of the mean, and what is left is $\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}(\cos^2\omega t + \sin^2\omega t)$ (transcript pp. 11–12):

<Formula label="Constant uncertainties" tex="(\Delta x)^2(t) = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}, \qquad (\Delta p)^2(t) = \frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}, \qquad \Delta x\,\Delta p = \frac{\hbar}{2}" cite="reading2" at="eqs. (4.38)–(4.42)" />

The uncertainty principle stays saturated at all times, so the state remains the same Gaussian, oscillating **coherently** without changing shape (transcript p. 12).

## The coherent state in the energy basis

Why does a superposition of energy eigenstates behave so well? The oscillator has a natural length (transcript p. 13):

<Formula label="Oscillator length" tex="d_0^2 = \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.43)" />

essentially the ground-state position uncertainty, up to $\sqrt{2}$ (the notes call it $d$). Writing $\hat p$ with $a$ and $a^\dagger$ turns the exponent into $\frac{x_0}{\sqrt{2}d_0}(a^\dagger - a)$, which is already anti-Hermitian, so no $i$ appears (transcript p. 13; notes eq. (4.48)). To split the creation and annihilation parts, use (transcript pp. 13–14)

<Formula label="Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff, special case" tex="e^{X + Y} = e^Xe^Ye^{-\frac{1}{2}[X, Y]} \quad \text{if } [X, Y] \text{ commutes with } X \text{ and } Y" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.49)" />

with $X$ the creation part and $Y$ the annihilation part, so that the annihilation operators stand next to the ground state and kill it. Their commutator is a number, so the formula applies, and (transcript p. 15)

<Formula label="Coherent state from creation operators" tex="\tilde\psi = e^{-x_0^2/4d_0^2}\,e^{\frac{x_0}{\sqrt{2}d_0}a^\dagger}\psi_0 = e^{-x_0^2/4d_0^2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\left(\frac{x_0}{\sqrt{2}d_0}\right)^n\psi_n" cite="reading2" at="eqs. (4.54)–(4.56)" />

(The transcript first starts the sum at $n = 1$ and then corrects it to $n = 0$, p. 17.) The probability of the $n$-th energy level is the square of the coefficient (transcript p. 16):

<Formula label="Poisson distribution of levels" tex="|c_n|^2 = e^{-\lambda}\,\frac{\lambda^n}{n!}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{x_0^2}{2d_0^2}" cite="reading2" at="eqs. (4.58)–(4.59)" />

This is the **Poisson distribution**, the one that counts, for example, radioactive decays in a fixed time; Zwiebach admits he finds it puzzling that it appears here (transcript pp. 16–17). The probabilities sum to $e^{-\lambda}e^\lambda = 1$, and the mean level is $\langle n\rangle = \lambda$, which is also $\langle\hat N\rangle$ in the coherent state (transcript pp. 17–18; notes eqs. (4.60)–(4.62)). So displacing the Gaussian by 1000 times $d_0$ occupies levels around half a million (the lecture says, loosely, a million). The energy spread is (transcript p. 18)

<Formula label="Energy uncertainty" tex="\Delta E = \hbar\omega\,\frac{x_0}{\sqrt{2}\,d_0}" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.69)" />

(the $\sqrt{2}$ was first missed and then added in the lecture). For a "classical" coherent state, $x_0 \gg d_0$, this spans many oscillator levels; yet compared with the mean energy above the ground state, $\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x_0^2$, it is small by the same large factor $x_0/d_0$ (up to $\sqrt{2}$), so the energy is relatively sharp (transcript pp. 18–19).

## The general coherent state

Allowing a **complex** parameter $\alpha$ gives the most useful family (transcript p. 19):

<Formula label="General coherent state" tex="\psi_\alpha = D(\alpha)\,\psi_0 = e^{\alpha a^\dagger - \alpha^*a}\,\psi_0" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.74)" />

$D(\alpha)$ is unitary because its exponent is anti-Hermitian, and real $\alpha = x_0/\sqrt{2}d_0$ gives back the displaced ground state. Since $a$ kills $\psi_0$, $a\psi_\alpha$ equals the commutator of $a$ with the exponential, which is $\alpha$ times the exponential (transcript p. 20):

<Formula label="Eigenstate of the annihilation operator" tex="a\,\psi_\alpha = \alpha\,\psi_\alpha" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.77)" />

A non-Hermitian operator has been "diagonalised", but the usual theorems do not apply: these eigenvectors with different eigenvalues are not orthogonal and do not form an orthonormal basis (transcript p. 20). The real and imaginary parts of $\alpha$ give the mean position and momentum (transcript pp. 20–21):

<Formula label="What α encodes" tex="\langle\hat x\rangle = \sqrt{2}\,d_0\,\text{Re}\,\alpha, \qquad \langle\hat p\rangle = \frac{\sqrt{2}\,\hbar}{d_0}\,\text{Im}\,\alpha" cite="reading2" at="eqs. (4.80)–(4.82)" />

So a complex $\alpha$ places the Gaussian and also gives it a kick. In time, $e^{-iHt/\hbar}$ can be passed through the exponential, turning $a$ and $a^\dagger$ into Heisenberg operators at $-t$, and (transcript pp. 22–23)

<Formula label="Time evolution of a coherent state" tex="\psi_\alpha(t) = e^{-i\omega t/2}\,\psi_{\beta}, \qquad \beta = \alpha e^{-i\omega t}" cite="reading2" at="eq. (4.93)" />

Apart from an overall phase, the state stays coherent and its parameter **rotates** in the complex plane with angular frequency $\omega$: the point $(\text{Re}\,\alpha, \text{Im}\,\alpha)$ traces the classical orbit in (scaled) position and momentum.

## Worked example

<WorkedExample title="A coherent state with α = 1 + 2i">
<Problem>
For $\psi_\alpha$ with $\alpha = 1 + 2i$, find $\langle\hat x\rangle$ and $\langle\hat p\rangle$ in terms of $d_0$, the mean number $\langle\hat N\rangle = |\alpha|^2$ and its spread $\Delta N = |\alpha|$, the probability of finding the ground state, and the mean position and momentum a quarter period later, at $\omega t = \pi/2$.
</Problem>
<Step title="Position and momentum">
$\langle\hat x\rangle = \sqrt{2}\,d_0 \times 1 = \sqrt{2}\,d_0$ and $\langle\hat p\rangle = \frac{\sqrt{2}\hbar}{d_0} \times 2 = \frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\hbar}{d_0}$: displaced to the right and moving to the right.
</Step>
<Step title="Occupation">
$\langle\hat N\rangle = |\alpha|^2 = 1 + 4 = 5$ and $\Delta N = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.24$ (notes eqs. (4.96)–(4.99)). The levels are Poisson distributed with mean 5 (notes eq. (4.94) gives the expansion for complex $\alpha$), so the ground state has probability $e^{-5} \approx 0.0067$.
</Step>
<Step title="A quarter period later">
$\alpha e^{-i\pi/2} = (1 + 2i)(-i) = 2 - i$. So $\langle\hat x\rangle = 2\sqrt{2}\,d_0$ and $\langle\hat p\rangle = -\frac{\sqrt{2}\hbar}{d_0}$: the particle has already passed its turning point (at $\omega t = \arctan 2 \approx 63.4°$, at the amplitude $\sqrt{2}\,d_0|\alpha| = \sqrt{10}\,d_0 \approx 3.16\,d_0$) and is moving back towards the centre, as a classical oscillator does. $|\alpha|$ and hence $\langle\hat N\rangle$ have not changed.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Summary

- $T_{x_0} = e^{-i\hat px_0/\hbar}$ is unitary, shifts $\hat x$ by $x_0$ and moves wavefunctions by $x_0$.
- The coherent state $T_{x_0}\psi_0$ is the displaced Gaussian, with energy $\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x_0^2$; in time $\langle\hat x\rangle = x_0\cos\omega t$ while $\Delta x$ and $\Delta p$ stay at their minimum values.
- In the energy basis the level probabilities are Poisson with mean $x_0^2/2d_0^2$, $d_0^2 = \hbar/m\omega$; the energy spread covers many levels but is small compared with the energy.
- The general coherent state $e^{\alpha a^\dagger - \alpha^*a}\psi_0$ satisfies $a\psi_\alpha = \alpha\psi_\alpha$; $\text{Re}\,\alpha$ and $\text{Im}\,\alpha$ give the mean position and momentum, and $\alpha$ rotates as $\alpha e^{-i\omega t}$.
