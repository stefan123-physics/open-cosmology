A paramagnet is a collection of independent magnetic moments that line up partly in an applied field. Its quantum version is a clean, exactly solvable example of the canonical ensemble, and also a warning about what the partition function really gives for magnetic systems (slides 1–26, numbered as printed, with 2a and 2b). (MIT's material for this lecture is Greytak's slides; this page follows them in its own words, with its own worked example.)

## The model

The moments come from electron spin and orbital angular momentum, $\vec\mu = g_J\mu_B\vec J$ with the Bohr magneton $\mu_B$ (built from the electron mass), or from nuclear spin, with the much smaller nuclear magneton built from the proton mass (slide 1). (The slides use Gaussian units, with $\mu_B = e\hbar/2m_ec$; in SI read $H$ as $\mu_0H$, the field in tesla, throughout.) In a field $H$ along $z$, the moment's projection is $g\mu_Bm$ with $m = J, J - 1, \dots, -J$: $2J + 1$ equally spaced levels, $g\mu_BH$ apart (slide 2a):

<Formula label="Energy levels" tex="\epsilon_m = -g\mu_BH\,m, \qquad m = J, J - 1, \dots, -J" cite="mit" at="slide 2a" />

With $\eta \equiv g\mu_BH/kT$, the level spacing in units of $kT$, the single-moment partition function is a geometric series (slide 2b):

<Formula label="One moment" tex="Z_1(T, H) = \sum_{m=-J}^{J}e^{\eta m} = \frac{\sinh\left[(J + \frac12)\eta\right]}{\sinh\left[\frac12\eta\right]}" cite="mit" at="slide 2b" />

$Z_1$ depends on $T$ and $H$ only through $\eta$, and so does $Z = Z_1^N$ at fixed $N$.

## Magnetization

The probability of level $m$ is $e^{\eta m}/Z_1$, and the mean moment is $\langle\mu\rangle = g\mu_B\,\partial\ln Z_1/\partial\eta$ (slide 3):

<Formula label="Magnetization and the Brillouin function" tex="M = N\langle\mu\rangle = Ng\mu_BJ\,B_J(\eta), \qquad B_J(\eta) = \frac1J\left[\left(J + \frac12\right)\text{coth}\left(\left(J + \frac12\right)\eta\right) - \frac12\,\text{coth}\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\right]" cite="mit" at="slide 3" />

$B_J$ is the **Brillouin function**. Using $\text{coth} x \approx 1/x + x/3$ for small $x$ and $\text{coth} x \approx 1 + 2e^{-2x}$ for large $x$ (slide 4):

<Formula label="Limits of the magnetization" tex="M \approx N\frac{(g\mu_B)^2J(J + 1)}{3}\frac{H}{kT}\ \ (kT \gg g\mu_BH), \qquad M \approx Ng\mu_BJ\left(1 - \frac1Je^{-\eta}\right)\ \ (kT \ll g\mu_BH)" cite="mit" at="slide 4" />

At high temperature, **Curie's law**: $M$ proportional to $H/T$, with the levels almost equally populated. At low temperature the moments are almost fully aligned, and the approach to saturation shows **energy-gap** behaviour (slides 5, 16).

## The energy puzzle

$M$ depends on $T$ and $H$ only through $\eta$, and so does the entropy $S$. Then $(\partial\eta/\partial T)_H = -\eta/T$ and $(\partial\eta/\partial H)_T = \eta/H$ (slide 6), and expanding $dU = T\,dS + H\,dM$ in $dT$ and $dH$ (slides 7–8):

<Formula label="The internal energy vanishes" tex="dU = \left[T\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}\right)_H + H\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial T}\right)_H\right]dT + \left[T\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial H}\right)_T + H\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial H}\right)_T\right]dH = 0" cite="mit" at="slide 7" />

Write $S'$ and $M'$ for the derivatives with respect to $\eta$. The Maxwell relation $(\partial S/\partial H)_T = (\partial M/\partial T)_H$ (from the magnetic Gibbs function below) gives $M' = -(T/H)S'$. Then the $dT$ bracket is $-\eta S' + \eta S' = 0$ and the $dH$ bracket is $(T\eta/H)(S' - S') = 0$. So $U = 0$ for this model on every path. The same follows physically (slide 9): heat changes $S$, adiabatic work changes $M$, and with $S$ and $M$ both functions of $\eta$, a change with $dS = 0$ also has $dM = 0$ and hence $dU = 0$.

Yet the average energy of the moments in the field is $N\langle\epsilon\rangle = -HM$, not zero. The resolution (slides 10–12): the total energy has three parts, the energy to create the field, the energy to assemble the moments, and the energy of the moments sitting in the field. The first does not appear when the work is written $H\,dM$; the second is zero here, since the moments already exist and do not interact; so $U = 0$, while the third, $-HM$, is what the Hamiltonian's average measures:

<Formula label="Average of the Hamiltonian" tex="\langle E\rangle = -\frac1Z\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\beta} = U - HM = -HM" cite="mit" at="slide 12" />

## Heat capacities

At constant magnetization $S$ is fixed (both depend on $\eta$ only), so $C_M = 0$. At constant field (slide 13),

<Formula label="Heat capacity at constant field" tex="C_H = -H\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial T}\right)_H = NkJ\eta^2B_J'(\eta)" cite="mit" at="slide 13" />

which vanishes at both ends of the temperature range, with a peak where $kT$ is comparable to the level spacing (slide 14).

## Entropy: which free energy?

By counting, the entropy should go from $Nk\ln(2J + 1)$ at high temperature (all levels equally likely) to $0$ at low temperature (one level). If one assumed $-kT\ln Z = F = U - TS$ with $U = 0$, then $S = k\ln Z = Nk\ln Z_1$: right at high temperature, but at low temperature it gives $NkJ\eta$, which grows without limit (slides 17–19).

The mistake is in the step $\langle E_1\rangle = U$ of the canonical derivation (slide 20). Here $\langle E\rangle = U - HM$, so

<Formula label="What the partition function gives" tex="-kT\ln Z = U - HM - TS = G(T, H), \qquad S = k\ln Z - \frac{HM}{T}" cite="mit" at="slide 20" />

and the extra term cancels the growth at low temperature, giving $S \to 0$. In general, for magnetic systems even when $U \ne 0$ (slide 21):

<Formula label="Magnetic Gibbs function" tex="G(T, H) = -kT\ln Z, \qquad dG = -S\,dT - M\,dH, \qquad M = -\left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial H}\right)_T, \qquad S = -\left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial T}\right)_H" cite="mit" at="slide 21" />

The paramagnet behaves like a sponge (slide 15): squeezing it with a field drives out entropy (as heat, if a bath is attached), and releasing the field lets it soak entropy back up.

## Cooling by adiabatic demagnetization

Because $S$ depends only on $H/T$ (slide 22), a paramagnetic salt can cool things (slides 23–24). First, in a strong field, cool it with a thermal link to an initial cooling stage at $T_{\text{initial}}$: with $kT \ll g\mu_BH$ the moments are aligned and the spin entropy is almost zero. Then break the link and slowly reduce the field. The total entropy cannot fall, so as $H$ drops the spins can only regain entropy by taking it from the lattice and from anything attached, which cools them. If the lattice and sample hold very little entropy compared with the spins (as at low temperature), the spins' entropy alone is nearly constant: $H/T$ stays fixed and $T$ falls in proportion to $H$, until interactions between moments, ignored in this model, take over near their ordering temperature. If instead the sample holds much entropy, the spins absorb as much as they can, ending near $Nk\ln(2J + 1)$ with $kT \gg g\mu_BH$, and the sample cools by less (slide 24).

Two real examples (slides 25–26): the electronic salt cerium magnesium nitrate (CMN), with ordering near 1.9 mK (its Ce³⁺ ions have $J = 5/2$ on the slide), cools helium-3 and samples in it to about 2 mK; the nuclear moments of metallic copper ($I = 3/2$), ordering near 1 μK, cool copper's electrons and lattice to about 10 μK.

<Problem>
Take electron spins with $J = \frac12$, $g = 2$, $\mu_B = 9.274 \times 10^{-24}$ J/T and $k = 1.380649 \times 10^{-23}$ J/K, in a field with $\mu_0H = 1$ T (so the level spacing is $g\mu_B \times 1$ T). (a) Find $\eta$ at 1 K and at 300 K. (b) Find $M/N\mu_B$ in each case and compare with Curie's law. (c) An ideal salt of such spins at 1 K is demagnetized adiabatically from 1 T to 0.01 T. What is its final temperature? (d) What is the largest entropy one mole of these spins can absorb?
</Problem>
<Step title="(a) η">
$\eta = 2 \times 9.274 \times 10^{-24}/(1.380649 \times 10^{-23}\,T)$: about 1.343 at 1 K and $4.48 \times 10^{-3}$ at 300 K.
</Step>
<Step title="(b) Magnetization">
For $J = \frac12$ the Brillouin function is $B_{1/2}(\eta) = \tanh(\eta/2)$, so $M/N\mu_B = gJ\tanh(\eta/2) = \tanh(\eta/2)$. At 1 K this is about 0.586, while Curie's law, $gJ(J + 1)\eta/3 = \eta/2$, would give 0.672: the spins are already well into saturation. At 300 K both give $2.24 \times 10^{-3}$: tiny, and exactly Curie.
</Step>
<Step title="(c) Demagnetization">
If the spins' entropy alone stays constant (nothing else holding entropy), $S$ depends on $H/T$ only, so $T_{\text{final}} = 1 \times 0.01/1 = 0.01$ K, in this ideal model.
</Step>
<Step title="(d) Maximum entropy">
$Nk\ln(2J + 1) = R\ln 2 \approx 5.76$ J/K per mole.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Summary

- Levels $\epsilon_m = -g\mu_BHm$; $Z_1 = \sinh[(J + \frac12)\eta]/\sinh(\eta/2)$ with $\eta = g\mu_BH/kT$.
- $M = Ng\mu_BJB_J(\eta)$ (Brillouin): Curie's law $M \propto H/T$ at high $T$, saturation at low.
- $U = 0$ in this model; $\langle E\rangle = -HM$ is the energy of the moments in the field; $-kT\ln Z = G(T, H)$ with $dG = -S\,dT - M\,dH$.
- $S$ runs from $0$ to $Nk\ln(2J + 1)$ and depends only on $H/T$: adiabatic demagnetization cools to millikelvin (CMN) and microkelvin (copper nuclei).
