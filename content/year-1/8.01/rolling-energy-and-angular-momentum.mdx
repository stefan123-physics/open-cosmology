The last lesson of the course puts the pieces of rolling together: the energy and the angular momentum of a body that both moves along and spins.

## Kinetic energy: translation plus rotation

Split a rolling wheel into elements $m_j$. Each moves with the centre's velocity $\vec V_{\text{cm}}$ plus its circular velocity $\vec v_{\text{cm},j}$ in the centre-of-mass frame (37.1 transcript, p. 1). Its kinetic energy is $\frac{1}{2}m_j\,\vec v_j \cdot \vec v_j$, and expanding the dot product gives three kinds of term (pp. 1–2):

<Formula label="Expanding the kinetic energy" tex="K = \frac{1}{2}\Big(\sum_j m_j\Big)V_{\text{cm}}^2 + \vec V_{\text{cm}} \cdot \sum_j m_j\vec v_{\text{cm},j} + \sum_j\frac{1}{2}m_j v_{\text{cm},j}^2" cite="reading2" at="p. 2" />

The middle term is zero: in the centre-of-mass frame the total momentum $\sum_j m_j\vec v_{\text{cm},j}$ vanishes, which is how that frame is defined (p. 3). In the last term each element moves in a circle, $v_{\text{cm},j} = r_j\omega$ with the same $\omega$ for all, so it becomes $\frac{1}{2}I_{\text{cm}}\omega^2$ (p. 3; notes, Sections 20.4–20.5, Eqs. (20.5.1)–(20.5.2), pp. 20-17–20-18):

<Formula label="Translational plus rotational kinetic energy" tex="K = \frac{1}{2}mV_{\text{cm}}^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{\text{cm}}\,\omega^2" cite="reading2" at="pp. 3–4" />

The first part is the **translational** kinetic energy, the motion of the centre; the second the **rotational** kinetic energy, the only kinetic energy seen in the centre-of-mass frame (p. 4).

## Rolling down an incline, by energy

A wheel rolls without slipping down an incline and drops a height $h$ (37.2 transcript, p. 1). Static friction acts at the contact point, but that point is instantaneously at rest, so $\vec f \cdot d\vec s = 0$: **static friction does no work**, the normal force does none either, and mechanical energy is conserved (p. 1). With $U = 0$ at the bottom and $\omega_f = v_f/R$ (pp. 1–2):

<Formula label="Speed at the bottom, by energy" tex="mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{\text{cm}}\frac{v_f^2}{R^2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad v_f = \sqrt{\frac{2mgh}{m + I_{\text{cm}}/R^2}}" cite="reading3" at="p. 2" />

the same as the torque method of the [previous lesson](/courses/8-01/rolling-dynamics), with much less work.

## Angular momentum: orbital plus spin

Now the angular momentum about a point P, for the same wheel. With $\vec R$ the vector from P to the centre of mass and $\vec r_{\text{cm},j}$ from the centre to element $j$, the position is $\vec r_j = \vec R + \vec r_{\text{cm},j}$ and the velocity $\vec v_j = \vec V_{\text{cm}} + \vec v_{\text{cm},j}$ (37.3 transcript, p. 1). The cross product gives four terms (pp. 1–2). Two of them vanish, because in the centre-of-mass frame both $\sum_j m_j\vec v_{\text{cm},j}$ and $\sum_j m_j\vec r_{\text{cm},j}$ are zero (pp. 1–2; notes, Eqs. (20.3.4)–(20.3.10), pp. 20-13–20-14). What remains is (pp. 2–3; notes, Eqs. (20.3.11)–(20.3.15), pp. 20-14–20-15):

<Formula label="Orbital plus spin angular momentum" tex="\vec L_P = \vec R \times m\vec V_{\text{cm}} + \sum_j \vec r_{\text{cm},j} \times m_j\vec v_{\text{cm},j} = \vec L^{\,\text{orbital}}_P + \vec L^{\,\text{spin}}_{\text{cm}}" cite="reading4" at="pp. 2–3" />

The first term treats the whole body as a point mass at its centre: the **orbital** angular momentum. The second is the angular momentum about the centre of mass, the **spin** (p. 3). For a body symmetric about its spin axis the spin is $I_{\text{cm}}\vec\omega$ (as segment 37.4 writes it). It is the same decomposition as for kinetic energy (p. 3).

## Fixed axis or translation and rotation?

The course summarises the two cases (segment 37.4, text page). For a rod pivoted at a fixed point S, use $\vec L_S = I_S\vec\omega$ and $K = \frac{1}{2}I_S\omega^2$. For a rod that translates and rotates, use $\vec L_S = \vec r_S \times m\vec v_{\text{cm}} + I_{\text{cm}}\vec\omega$ and $K = \frac{1}{2}mv_{\text{cm}}^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{\text{cm}}\omega^2$. The second forms also hold for the pivoted rod, since its centre moves in a circle and the parallel axis theorem turns them into the first, but to avoid confusion the course advises the first forms for fixed-axis rotation (text page).

<WorkedExample title="A bowling ball and a cylinder">
<Problem>
(a) A uniform 7.0 kg bowling ball of radius 0.11 m rolls without slipping at 8.0 m/s. Find its translational and rotational kinetic energy, and its angular momentum about a point on the floor directly below its path. (b) A uniform cylinder rolls without slipping down from a height of 1.0 m. How fast is it going, and what fraction of its kinetic energy is rotational?
</Problem>
<Step title="(a) Energy">
$K_{\text{trans}} = \frac{1}{2} \times 7.0 \times 8.0^2 = 224$ J. With $I_{\text{cm}} = \frac{2}{5}mR^2$ and $\omega = v/R$, $K_{\text{rot}} = \frac{2}{5} \times 224 \approx 89.6$ J, so the total is about 314 J.
</Step>
<Step title="(a) Angular momentum">
Orbital: the centre is at height $R$ above the floor, so $|\vec R \times m\vec V_{\text{cm}}| = mvR = 7.0 \times 8.0 \times 0.11 \approx 6.16$ kg m²/s. Spin: $I_{\text{cm}}\omega = \frac{2}{5}mR^2 \cdot \frac{v}{R} = \frac{2}{5}mvR \approx 2.46$ kg m²/s, in the same direction for a ball rolling forward. The total is $\frac{7}{5}mvR \approx 8.62$ kg m²/s: exactly $I\omega$ with $I = \frac{7}{5}mR^2$, the moment of inertia about the contact point, which is momentarily at rest.
</Step>
<Step title="(b) The cylinder">
$v = \sqrt{\frac{2gh}{1 + \frac{1}{2}}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{3} \times 9.8 \times 1.0} \approx 3.6$ m/s. The rotational part is $\frac{\frac{1}{2}}{1 + \frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{3}$ of the kinetic energy.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

<Callout kind="note" title="The Earth's orbit and spin">
The notes apply the orbital-plus-spin split to the Earth (notes, Example 20.5, pp. 20-15–20-17). Its orbital angular momentum about the Sun is about $2.7 \times 10^{40}$ kg m²/s, its spin angular momentum about $7.1 \times 10^{33}$ kg m²/s (treating the Earth as a uniform sphere): the orbital part is about 3.8 million times larger, because the ratio grows like the square of the distance to the Sun over the Earth's radius. (The two do not point the same way: the Earth's axis is tilted about 23.5° to its orbit.)
</Callout>

## Summary

- Kinetic energy: $K = \frac{1}{2}mV_{\text{cm}}^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{\text{cm}}\omega^2$, translational plus rotational.
- Static friction on a body rolling without slipping does no work: use energy conservation.
- Angular momentum: $\vec L_P = \vec R \times m\vec V_{\text{cm}} + \vec L_{\text{cm}}^{\,\text{spin}}$, orbital plus spin.
- For rotation about a fixed axis, $\vec L_S = I_S\vec\omega$ and $K = \frac{1}{2}I_S\omega^2$ are the simplest forms.
