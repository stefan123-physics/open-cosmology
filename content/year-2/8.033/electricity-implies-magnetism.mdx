## A short history

The history slides, borrowed from MIT's 8.02 course (slides 7–10), run from ancient Greece, where rubbed amber (in Greek, "elektron") attracted light objects and lodestone from Magnesia attracted iron, through du Fay's two kinds of charge (1730) and the inverse-square law of Priestley, Cavendish and Coulomb (1766–1786; the slide gives its precision as better than 2 parts in $10^9$, a level reached in the 1930s; later experiments pushed it much further), to Volta's battery (1800). Until then electricity and magnetism were separate subjects. Ørsted and Ampère connected them (1820), Faraday discovered magnetic induction (1831), Maxwell wrote down his equations (1873), Hertz linked electromagnetism with radiation (1887), and in 1905 Einstein's special relativity made the connection between electricity and magnetism "as natural as it can be".

**Coulomb's law** (slide 11). The force on charge $q_2$ from charge $q_1$ a distance $r$ away is

<Formula label="Coulomb's law" tex="\vec F_{12} = k_e\frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}\,\hat r, \qquad k_e = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \approx 8.9875 \times 10^9\ \text{N m}^2/\text{C}^2" cite="mit" at="slide 11" />

with $\hat r$ the unit vector from $q_1$ to $q_2$. Slide 12 pictures the potential of a positive and a negative charge as a landscape. Why of this form? Relativity answers part of that question (slide 11).

## From electricity to magnetism

The argument (slides 13–16) starts from what we know: a charge $q$ in an electric field $\vec E$ feels the force $\vec F = q\vec E$, whatever its velocity $\vec u$. The same law can be written with 4-vectors:

<Formula label="Electric force with 4-vectors" tex="Q = \frac{q}{c}MU, \qquad M = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & E_x \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & E_y \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & E_z \\ E_x & E_y & E_z & 0 \end{pmatrix}" cite="mit" at="slide 13" />

where $Q$ is the [force 4-vector](/courses/8-033/force-acceleration-interstellar-travel) (an outlined F on the slides) $\gamma_u(\vec F, \dot E/c)$ and $U = \gamma_u(\vec u, c)$ the velocity 4-vector. Its first three components give $\vec F = q\vec E$; its fourth, $\dot E = q\vec E\cdot\vec u = \vec F\cdot\vec u$, the power, as it should be (slide 13).

Now transform to a frame $S'$ moving at $v = \beta c$ along $x$ (slide 14). Since $Q' = \Lambda Q$ and $U' = \Lambda U$,

<Formula label="Transforming the field matrix" tex="Q' = \Lambda Q = \frac{q}{c}\Lambda MU = \frac{q}{c}\Lambda M\Lambda^{-1}\Lambda U = \frac{q}{c}M'U', \qquad M' \equiv \Lambda M\Lambda^{-1}" cite="mit" at="slide 14" />

Working out $M'$ for the matrix above shows two things (slides 14 and 16). First, the electric field picks up factors of $\gamma$: $E_x' = E_x$, while $E_y' = \gamma E_y$ and $E_z' = \gamma E_z$. Second, new entries appear in the upper left $3 \times 3$ block, which do not correspond to an electric field. That block stays antisymmetric however we transform, so it holds three new numbers, $M_{23}$, $M_{31}$ and $M_{12}$. Call them $B_x$, $B_y$ and $B_z$:

<Formula label="The field matrix" tex="M = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & B_z & -B_y & E_x \\ -B_z & 0 & B_x & E_y \\ B_y & -B_x & 0 & E_z \\ E_x & E_y & E_z & 0 \end{pmatrix}" cite="mit" at="slide 16" />

Putting this $M$ back into $Q = (q/c)MU$ gives the famous **Lorentz force law** of 8.02 (slides 16 and 18):

<Formula label="Lorentz force law" tex="\vec F = q\left(\vec E + \frac1c\,\vec u \times \vec B\right)" cite="mit" at="slide 18" />

and the fourth component again gives $\dot E = q\vec E\cdot\vec u$. So a field that is purely electric for one observer exerts, for another, a force that grows with the particle's speed: that velocity-dependent part is what we call magnetism (slide 16). (Slide 16 writes the law as $\vec F = q(\vec E + \vec u \times \vec B)$, without the $1/c$; with $B$ defined as above it needs the $1/c$, as in the summary on slide 18. Defined this way, $B$ has the same units as $E$: it is $c$ times the SI magnetic field, as in Gaussian units. In SI units the law reads $\vec F = q(\vec E + \vec u \times \vec B)$.)

**Transforming any field** (slides 17–18). With the full $M$, $M' = \Lambda M\Lambda^{-1}$ gives, for a boost along $x$:

<Formula label="Lorentz transformation of the field" tex="E_x' = E_x, \quad E_y' = \gamma\left(E_y - \beta B_z\right), \quad E_z' = \gamma\left(E_z + \beta B_y\right), \quad B_x' = B_x, \quad B_y' = \gamma\left(B_y + \beta E_z\right), \quad B_z' = \gamma\left(B_z - \beta E_y\right)" cite="mit" at="slide 18" />

The components along the motion are unchanged; those across it mix, each with a factor $\gamma$. Electric and magnetic fields are not separate things but parts of one object, whose split into $\vec E$ and $\vec B$ depends on the observer.

## The current 4-vector and invariants

**Charge and current** (slides 19 and 21). Electromagnetism has two parts: how fields affect matter, which is the Lorentz force above, and how matter affects fields, which comes next. The theory of gravity has the same two parts; in general relativity both become geometry: matter moves along geodesics through spacetime, and matter curves spacetime (slide 19; the slide's "former" and "latter" are swapped there, and the page gives the intended pairing). What produces the fields is charge, spread through spacetime with a density $\rho(\vec r, t)$ and flowing with a current density $\vec J(\vec r, t)$. Together they form the **current 4-vector**, written $J_4$ on this page (an outlined J on the slides; slide 21):

<Formula label="Current 4-vector" tex="J_4 \equiv \begin{pmatrix} J_x \\ J_y \\ J_z \\ \rho c \end{pmatrix} = \rho_0U" cite="mit" at="slide 21" />

The second form applies to a uniformly charged lump moving with 4-velocity $U$, whose density measured in its own rest frame is $\rho_0$, the *proper charge density*. Several kinds of moving charge, electrons drifting one way and ions the other in a wire for example, give a total 4-current that is just the sum of the separate ones (slide 21).

**What is Lorentz invariant?** (slide 20) The slide's table compares classical and relativistic physics. Charge, spin and lepton number are the same in every frame in both: they are integers (in units of the electron charge, of $\hbar/2$ and of one lepton). Duration, length and mass are frame-independent classically but not relativistically (mass here in the sense of the "relativistic mass" $\gamma m_0$); proper duration, proper length and rest mass are invariant in both; momentum and energy are invariant in neither.

<Callout kind="cosmology" title="Two halves, in gravity too">
The slides' analogy, matter moves through spacetime along geodesics and matter curves spacetime, is exactly how cosmology works. For a uniform universe, "matter curves spacetime" becomes the [Friedmann equation](/courses/8-286/friedmann-equation), which ties the expansion rate to the density of matter and energy.
</Callout>

<WorkedExample title="A field seen from a moving frame, and a moving blob">
<Problem>
(a) In the lab there is a uniform electric field $E_y = 1.0 \times 10^6$ V/m and no magnetic field. What does an observer moving at $0.6c$ along $x$ measure, in SI units? (b) A blob of charge has proper density $\rho_0 = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}$ C/m³ and moves at $0.8c$. Find $\rho$, $J$ and check the invariant.
</Problem>
<Step title="(a) A magnetic field appears">
$\gamma = 1.25$, so $E_y' = 1.25 \times 10^6$ V/m. In SI units the magnetic field is $B_z' = -\gamma\beta E_y/c = -1.25 \times 0.6 \times 10^6/(3.00 \times 10^8) = -2.5 \times 10^{-3}$ T.
</Step>
<Step title="(b) The 4-current">
$\gamma = 1/0.6 \approx 1.67$, so $\rho = \gamma\rho_0 \approx 1.67 \times 10^{-6}$ C/m³ and $J = \rho u = 1.67 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.8 \times 3.00 \times 10^8 \approx 400$ A/m² (exactly 400 with $\gamma = 5/3$). Then $\rho c = 500$ A/m², and $(\rho c)^2 - J^2 = 500^2 - 400^2 = 300^2 = (\rho_0c)^2$: the same in every frame.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Summary

- Writing $\vec F = q\vec E$ as $Q = (q/c)MU$ and Lorentz transforming produces new field components: the magnetic field, and the Lorentz force law $\vec F = q(\vec E + \frac1c\vec u \times \vec B)$.
- $\vec E$ and $\vec B$ transform into each other; the components along the boost stay the same.
- Charge, spin and lepton number are invariant; charge and current density form the 4-vector $J_4 = (\vec J, \rho c) = \rho_0U$.
