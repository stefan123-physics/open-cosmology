Two simple potentials show how confinement quantizes energy. Between hard walls the energies and states can be written down at once; in a well of finite depth they come from a pair of equations best solved with a picture (notes pp. 1–10). (MIT's material for this lecture is Zwiebach's lecture notes, Chapter 11; this page follows their order in its own words, with its own worked example. The notes' figures are not reproduced.)

## The infinite square well

The potential confines the particle to $0 < x < a$ with hard walls at both ends (notes p. 1):

<Formula label="Infinite square well" tex="V(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & 0 < x < a \\ \infty, & x \le 0 \text{ or } x \ge a \end{cases}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 1, eq. (1.1)" />

Classically a particle can never be where the potential exceeds its energy. Quantum mechanics allows it, but not into an infinite potential: $\psi = 0$ outside the interval, which the finite well will justify as a limit (notes p. 1, eq. (1.2)). Since $\psi$ is continuous, the boundary conditions are $\psi(0) = 0$ and $\psi(a) = 0$. Nothing is imposed on $\psi'$: a solution of a second-order equation with both $\psi$ and $\psi'$ zero at a point vanishes everywhere, so at a hard wall the slope must be allowed to jump (notes p. 2).

Inside, $\psi'' = -\frac{2mE}{\hbar^2}\psi$, and (as on the circle) $E > 0$, so with $k^2 = 2mE/\hbar^2$ the general solution is $c_1\cos kx + c_2\sin kx$ (notes p. 2, eqs. (1.3)–(1.6)). $\psi(0) = 0$ removes the cosine; $\psi(a) = 0$ then needs $\sin ka = 0$, so $k_n = n\pi/a$ (eqs. (1.7)–(1.8)). Here $n = 0$ gives nothing, and $-n$ only flips the sign, so $n$ runs over the positive integers. Normalizing, $\int_0^a N^2\sin^2(n\pi x/a)\,dx = N^2a/2 = 1$ (notes pp. 2–3, eq. (1.10)), which gives

<Formula label="States and energies of the infinite square well" tex="\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac2a}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}, \qquad E_n = \frac{\hbar^2k_n^2}{2m} = \frac{\hbar^2\pi^2n^2}{2ma^2}, \qquad n = 1, 2, \dots" cite="mit" at="notes p. 3, eq. (1.11)" />

Every $n$ gives a different energy, so there are **no degeneracies**, and the ground state ($n = 1$) has a nonzero energy (notes p. 3). Features of the states (notes pp. 3–4):

1. The ground state has no **nodes** (zeros inside the interval; the zeros at the walls do not count). This holds for the normalizable ground state of any one-dimensional potential.
2. Each level up adds one node: $\psi_n$ has $n - 1$ nodes. The first excited state's single node is at the midpoint $a/2$.
3. About the midpoint the states are alternately symmetric and antisymmetric. That is no accident: shifting the well to $-a/2 < x < a/2$ makes the potential even, and the bound states of an even one-dimensional potential are always even or odd (proved in a later lecture).
4. The $\psi_n$ form a complete set for functions on $[0, a]$ that vanish at the ends. A function that does not vanish there can be expanded too, but delicately, and as a wavefunction it would have infinite expected energy.

## The finite square well

Now a well of depth $V_0 > 0$ and width $2a$, centred on the origin (notes p. 4):

<Formula label="Finite square well" tex="V(x) = \begin{cases} -V_0, & |x| \le a \\ 0, & |x| > a \end{cases}, \qquad V_0 > 0" cite="mit" at="notes p. 4, eq. (2.12)" />

The bottom of the well is now at $-V_0$, so energies must be compared with care: the infinite well's are measured from its bottom. **Bound states** are normalizable energy eigenstates, and they need $-V_0 < E < 0$: with $E > 0$ the solution outside would be a wave extending to infinity (notes p. 5, eq. (2.13)). The energy measured from the bottom is $\tilde E = V_0 - |E| > 0$, and these are the energies to compare with the infinite well as $V_0 \to \infty$ (eq. (2.14)).

The equation $\psi'' = -\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E - V)\psi$ has a positive constant multiplying $\psi$ outside the well (real exponentials) and a negative one inside (sines and cosines) (notes pp. 5–6, eq. (2.15)). The potential is even, so the bound states are even or odd.

**Even solutions** (notes pp. 6–7). Inside, $\psi'' = -k^2\psi$ with $k^2 = 2m(V_0 - |E|)/\hbar^2$, the usual relation between wavenumber and kinetic energy; the even solution is $\cos kx$ (no normalization needed to find the energies). Outside, $\psi'' = \kappa^2\psi$ with $\kappa^2 = 2m|E|/\hbar^2$, and normalizability demands the decaying exponential, $Ae^{-\kappa|x|}$: far from the well the particle is very unlikely to be found (eqs. (2.16)–(2.24)). The energy drops out of the sum (notes p. 7):

<Formula label="Wavenumbers inside and outside" tex="k^2 + \kappa^2 = \frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 7, eq. (2.25)" />

With the unit-free numbers $\xi \equiv ka$, $\eta \equiv \kappa a$ and the well's own constant (notes p. 7)

<Formula label="The strength of the well" tex="z_0^2 \equiv \frac{2mV_0a^2}{\hbar^2}, \qquad \xi^2 + \eta^2 = z_0^2" cite="mit" at="notes p. 7, eqs. (2.26)–(2.27)" />

$\xi$ and $\eta$ depend on the energy, while $z_0$ depends only on the depth, the width and the particle's mass: deep or wide wells have large $z_0$. The energy in terms of these (notes pp. 7–8):

<Formula label="Energy from η or ξ" tex="\frac{|E|}{V_0} = \frac{\eta^2}{z_0^2}, \qquad \tilde E = V_0 - |E| = \xi^2\,\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2}" cite="mit" at="notes pp. 7–8, eqs. (2.29) and (2.31)" />

The second form is $\xi^2$ times the energy scale of an infinite well of width $a$. The last step is matching at $x = a$: continuity of $\psi$ gives $\cos ka = Ae^{-\kappa a}$ and of $\psi'$ gives $-k\sin ka = -\kappa Ae^{-\kappa a}$. Dividing removes $A$ and leaves $k\tan ka = \kappa$ (notes p. 8, eqs. (2.32)–(2.33)). So the even bound states solve

<Formula label="Even bound states" tex="\xi^2 + \eta^2 = z_0^2, \qquad \eta = \xi\tan\xi, \qquad \xi, \eta > 0" cite="mit" at="notes p. 8, eq. (2.34)" />

**Odd solutions** (notes pp. 8–9). With $\sin kx$ inside and a decaying exponential outside (times the sign of $x$, to make it odd; the notes' eq. (2.35) leaves out that sign and writes the exponent as $-k|x|$, a slip for $-\kappa|x|$), the matching gives instead

<Formula label="Odd bound states" tex="\xi^2 + \eta^2 = z_0^2, \qquad \eta = -\xi\cot\xi, \qquad \xi, \eta > 0" cite="mit" at="notes p. 8, eq. (2.36)" />

**Reading the picture** (notes pp. 8–9). The circle has radius $z_0$; the even curves rise from zero to infinity as $\xi$ approaches each odd multiple of $\pi/2$; the odd curves start at $\xi = \pi/2, 3\pi/2, \dots$ and also rise to infinity, at each multiple of $\pi$. Each crossing is a bound state. The **ground state** is the crossing with the smallest $\xi$ (largest $\eta$), the most deeply bound. A larger $z_0$ gives more crossings: $z_0$ controls the number of bound states. Two facts stand out: there is **always at least one even bound state**, however shallow or narrow the well, because the circle always meets the first even curve; and there is no odd bound state unless $z_0 > \pi/2$.

**Why the energy is quantized** (notes pp. 9–10). Fix an energy and try to solve. With the inside coefficient set to 1, the only unknown is $A$, but there are two conditions, continuity of $\psi$ and of $\psi'$. One unknown and two equations generally have no solution. Counting $E$ as a second unknown, two equations in two unknowns can be solved, at special energies only.

**Shapes** (notes p. 10). For a well with three bound states, the states alternate even, odd, even, with zero, one and two nodes. Inside the well $\psi''$ has the opposite sign to $\psi$ (curving towards the axis), outside the same sign (curving away), with a jump in $\psi''$ at $x = \pm a$. Outside, the ground state decays fastest and the least bound state slowest.

<WorkedExample title="An electron in a 10 eV well">
<Problem>
An electron ($m_ec^2 = 511{,}000$ eV, $\hbar c = 197.33$ eV nm) is in a finite well of depth $V_0 = 10$ eV and width $2a = 0.40$ nm. (a) Find $z_0$ and the number of bound states. (b) The crossings are at $\xi \approx 1.194$, $2.336$ and $3.228$ (found numerically). Find the three energies. (c) Compare the ground state with that of an infinite well of the same width, $0.40$ nm.
</Problem>
<Step title="(a) The strength of the well">
$z_0 = \frac{a\sqrt{2m_ec^2V_0}}{\hbar c} = \frac{0.20\sqrt{2 \times 511{,}000 \times 10}}{197.33} \approx 3.24$. Even states: $z_0$ passes $0$ and $\pi$, so two. Odd states: $z_0$ passes $\pi/2$ but not $3\pi/2$, so one. Three bound states in all.
</Step>
<Step title="(b) The energies">
$\eta = \sqrt{z_0^2 - \xi^2}$ and $E = -V_0\eta^2/z_0^2$: for $\xi = 1.194$, $\eta \approx 3.01$ and $E \approx -8.64$ eV; for $\xi = 2.336$, $\eta \approx 2.25$ and $E \approx -4.80$ eV; for $\xi = 3.228$, $\eta \approx 0.28$ and $E \approx -0.07$ eV, barely bound.
</Step>
<Step title="(c) Finite against infinite">
Measured from the bottom, the ground state has $\tilde E = 10 - 8.64 \approx 1.36$ eV. An infinite well of width $0.40$ nm has $E_1 = \frac{\pi^2(\hbar c)^2}{2m_ec^2(0.40)^2} \approx 2.35$ eV. The finite well's level is lower: its wavefunction leaks into the walls, so it is effectively wider and needs less curvature.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Summary

- Infinite well on $[0, a]$: $\psi_n = \sqrt{2/a}\sin(n\pi x/a)$, $E_n = \hbar^2\pi^2n^2/(2ma^2)$; no degeneracy, $n - 1$ nodes, alternating symmetry; $\psi'$ jumps at the walls.
- Finite well of depth $V_0$ and width $2a$: bound states have $-V_0 < E < 0$, oscillate inside and decay outside.
- They solve $\xi^2 + \eta^2 = z_0^2$ with $\eta = \xi\tan\xi$ (even) or $\eta = -\xi\cot\xi$ (odd), where $z_0^2 = 2mV_0a^2/\hbar^2$ fixes their number.
- There is always at least one bound state; energy is quantized because matching both $\psi$ and $\psi'$ needs the energy as a second unknown.
