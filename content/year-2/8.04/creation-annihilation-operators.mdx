The previous lecture found the oscillator's energies by solving a differential equation as a power series. Here the same results come from algebra: the Hamiltonian is factorized into a product of two first-order operators, and everything else follows from their commutator (notes pp. 1–8). (MIT's material for these two lectures is Zwiebach's combined lecture notes for Lectures 14 and 15; this page follows their order in its own words, with its own worked example. The site's numbering follows MIT's calendar, in which this material is Lecture 15.)

## Factorizing the Hamiltonian

Write the Hamiltonian as (notes p. 1)

<Formula label="Oscillator Hamiltonian" tex="\hat H = \frac12m\omega^2\left(\hat x^2 + \frac{\hat p^2}{m^2\omega^2}\right)" cite="mit" at="notes p. 1, eq. (1.1)" />

For numbers, $a^2 + b^2 = (a - ib)(a + ib)$. For operators the product picks up an extra term, because $\hat x$ and $\hat p$ do not commute (notes p. 1):

<Formula label="An almost-factorization" tex="\left(\hat x - \frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\right)\left(\hat x + \frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\right) = \hat x^2 + \frac{\hat p^2}{m^2\omega^2} + \frac{i}{m\omega}\left[\hat x, \hat p\right] = \hat x^2 + \frac{\hat p^2}{m^2\omega^2} - \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 1, eq. (1.2)" />

Call the right-hand factor $\hat V \equiv \hat x + i\hat p/(m\omega)$. Since $\hat x$ and $\hat p$ are Hermitian, the left-hand factor is its Hermitian conjugate, $\hat V^\dagger = \hat x - i\hat p/(m\omega)$, and so (notes p. 1, eqs. (1.3)–(1.6))

<Formula label="Hamiltonian in terms of V" tex="\hat H = \frac12m\omega^2\,\hat V^\dagger\hat V + \frac12\hbar\omega" cite="mit" at="notes p. 1, eq. (1.6)" />

Their commutator is $[\hat V, \hat V^\dagger] = 2\hbar/(m\omega)$ (eq. (1.7)), which suggests rescaling to unit-free operators, the **annihilation operator** $\hat a$ and the **creation operator** $\hat a^\dagger$ (notes p. 2):

<Formula label="Annihilation and creation operators" tex="\hat a = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x + \frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\right), \qquad \hat a^\dagger = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x - \frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\right), \qquad \left[\hat a, \hat a^\dagger\right] = 1" cite="mit" at="notes p. 2, eqs. (1.9)–(1.11)" />

Neither is Hermitian; each is the other's conjugate. Inverting (notes p. 2):

<Formula label="Position and momentum from the ladder operators" tex="\hat x = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\hat a + \hat a^\dagger\right), \qquad \hat p = i\sqrt{\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}}\left(\hat a^\dagger - \hat a\right)" cite="mit" at="notes p. 2, eq. (1.12)" />

and the Hamiltonian becomes (notes p. 2)

<Formula label="Factorized Hamiltonian" tex="\hat H = \hbar\omega\left(\hat a^\dagger\hat a + \frac12\right) = \hbar\omega\left(\hat N + \frac12\right), \qquad \hat N \equiv \hat a^\dagger\hat a" cite="mit" at="notes p. 2, eq. (1.14)" />

$\hat N$, the **number operator**, is Hermitian, and its eigenvalues $N$ give the energies $E = \hbar\omega(N + \frac12)$ (eq. (1.15)).

## Two consequences of factorizing

**No energy below $\hbar\omega/2$** (notes p. 3). In any normalized state, moving $\hat a^\dagger$ to the first slot of the inner product gives

<Formula label="A lower bound on the energy" tex="\langle\hat H\rangle = \hbar\omega\left(\left(\hat a\psi, \hat a\psi\right) + \frac12\right) \ge \frac12\hbar\omega" cite="mit" at="notes p. 3, eqs. (1.16)–(1.18)" />

since $(\hat a\psi, \hat a\psi)$, a squared norm, is never negative. So every energy eigenvalue is at least $\hbar\omega/2$.

**The ground state from a first-order equation** (notes p. 3). The bound is reached only if $\hat a\psi = 0$. So the ground state is annihilated by $\hat a$, which explains its name. With $\hat p = \frac{\hbar}{i}\frac{d}{dx}$ this reads $\left(x + \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\psi_0 = 0$, a **first-order** equation, far simpler than the Schrödinger equation (eqs. (1.19)–(1.21)). Its normalized solution (notes p. 3):

<Formula label="Oscillator ground state" tex="\psi_0(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}e^{-\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}x^2}, \qquad E_0 = \frac12\hbar\omega" cite="mit" at="notes p. 3, eqs. (1.22)–(1.23)" />

**Factorization in general** (notes p. 4). Whenever a Hamiltonian can be written as $\hat H = \hat A^\dagger\hat A + E_0$ for some operator $\hat A$, the same two arguments apply: $\langle\hat H\rangle = (\hat A\psi, \hat A\psi) + E_0 \ge E_0$, so no energy lies below $E_0$; and a state with $\hat A\psi_0 = 0$ is an eigenstate with energy exactly $E_0$, the ground state (eqs. (1.24)–(1.30)).

## Climbing the ladder

**Commutators** (notes pp. 4–5). Since $\hat N\psi_0 = 0$ (notes p. 4, eq. (2.1)), and

<Formula label="Number operator with the ladder operators" tex="\left[\hat N, \hat a\right] = -\hat a, \qquad \left[\hat N, \hat a^\dagger\right] = \hat a^\dagger" cite="mit" at="notes p. 5, eq. (2.3)" />

$\hat N$ counts: by induction $[\hat N, (\hat a^\dagger)^k] = k(\hat a^\dagger)^k$ and $[\hat N, \hat a^k] = -k\hat a^k$, and similarly $[\hat a, (\hat a^\dagger)^k] = k(\hat a^\dagger)^{k-1}$ and $[\hat a^\dagger, \hat a^k] = -k\,\hat a^{k-1}$ (eqs. (2.4)–(2.5)). A useful lemma: **if $\hat A\psi = 0$, then $\hat A\hat B\psi = [\hat A, \hat B]\psi$**, because $\hat A\hat B = [\hat A, \hat B] + \hat B\hat A$ and the last term kills $\psi$ (notes p. 5, eqs. (2.6)–(2.8)).

**The first excited state** (notes pp. 5–6). $\hat a^\dagger$ cannot also annihilate $\psi_0$: applying $[\hat a, \hat a^\dagger] = 1$ to $\psi_0$ would then give $0 = \psi_0$. So take $\psi_1 \equiv \hat a^\dagger\psi_0$. By the lemma, $\hat N\psi_1 = [\hat N, \hat a^\dagger]\psi_0 = \hat a^\dagger\psi_0 = \psi_1$: an eigenstate with $N = 1$ and $E_1 = \frac32\hbar\omega$ (eqs. (2.9)–(2.12)). The creation operator, also called the **raising operator**, raised $N$ by one. It is even normalized: $(\psi_1, \psi_1) = (\psi_0, \hat a\hat a^\dagger\psi_0) = (\psi_0, [\hat a, \hat a^\dagger]\psi_0) = 1$ (eqs. (2.13)–(2.14)).

**Higher states** (notes p. 6). $\hat a^\dagger\hat a^\dagger\psi_0$ has $N = 2$ and squared norm 2, so the normalized state carries $1/\sqrt2$ (eqs. (2.15)–(2.18)). In general (notes p. 6):

<Formula label="The nth excited state" tex="\psi_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\left(\hat a^\dagger\right)^n\psi_0, \qquad E_n = \hbar\omega\left(n + \frac12\right)" cite="mit" at="notes p. 6, eqs. (2.19)–(2.20)" />

These are eigenstates of the Hermitian $\hat H$ with different eigenvalues, so they are orthonormal, $(\psi_n, \psi_m) = \delta_{mn}$ (notes p. 7, eq. (2.21)). The ladder operators move between them (notes p. 7):

<Formula label="Ladder relations" tex="\hat a\,\psi_n = \sqrt n\,\psi_{n-1}, \qquad \hat a^\dagger\psi_n = \sqrt{n + 1}\,\psi_{n+1}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 7, eqs. (2.22)–(2.25)" />

$\hat a$ lowers the number by one, except on the ground state, which it kills; $\hat a^\dagger$ raises it by one.

## Uncertainty in position, by algebra

In $\psi_n$, $\langle\hat x\rangle = 0$, since $x$ is odd and $|\psi_n|^2$ is even; explicitly, writing $\hat x \propto \hat a + \hat a^\dagger$, the two terms turn $\psi_n$ into $\psi_{n\pm1}$, both orthogonal to $\psi_n$ (notes p. 7, eqs. (2.26)–(2.27)). For $\hat x^2 \propto (\hat a + \hat a^\dagger)^2$, the terms $\hat a\hat a$ and $\hat a^\dagger\hat a^\dagger$ give $\psi_{n\pm2}$ and drop out, leaving $\hat a\hat a^\dagger + \hat a^\dagger\hat a = 1 + 2\hat N$ (notes pp. 7–8, eqs. (2.28)–(2.30)). So

<Formula label="Position uncertainty in the nth state" tex="\left(\Delta x\right)_n^2 = \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\left(n + \frac12\right)" cite="mit" at="notes p. 8, eq. (2.31)" />

It is the square of the uncertainty that grows linearly with $n$; $\Delta x$ itself grows like $\sqrt{n + \frac12}$. (The notes say "the position uncertainty grows linearly", meaning its square.)

<Callout kind="cosmology" title="Oscillators everywhere, even in empty space">
A quantum field in a box behaves as a collection of independent harmonic oscillators, one for each standing wave, and each keeps its zero-point energy $\frac12\hbar\omega$ even in its ground state. Summed over all modes this vacuum energy is enormous, the starting point of the cosmological constant problem. [8.286 explains the puzzle of the vacuum energy](/courses/8-286/vacuum-energy-puzzle).
</Callout>

<WorkedExample title="Ladders and uncertainties">
<Problem>
(a) Using $\hat p \propto \hat a^\dagger - \hat a$, find $(\Delta p)_n^2$, and the product $\Delta x\,\Delta p$ in $\psi_n$. (b) For an electron in an oscillator with $\hbar\omega = 1.0$ eV, find $\Delta x$ in $\psi_0$ and $\psi_3$ ($\hbar c = 197.33$ eV nm, $m_ec^2 = 511{,}000$ eV). (c) Show that the average potential energy in $\psi_n$ is half the total energy.
</Problem>
<Step title="(a) Momentum">
$\hat p^2 = -\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}(\hat a^\dagger - \hat a)^2$; the $\hat a\hat a$ and $\hat a^\dagger\hat a^\dagger$ terms drop out and $-(\hat a^\dagger - \hat a)^2 \to \hat a^\dagger\hat a + \hat a\hat a^\dagger = 1 + 2\hat N$, so $(\Delta p)_n^2 = m\omega\hbar\left(n + \frac12\right)$ (and $\langle\hat p\rangle = 0$). Then $\Delta x\,\Delta p = \hbar\left(n + \frac12\right)$: the ground state has exactly $\hbar/2$, the smallest product Heisenberg allows.
</Step>
<Step title="(b) Numbers">
$(\Delta x)_0^2 = \frac{(\hbar c)^2}{2m_ec^2\,\hbar\omega} = \frac{197.33^2}{2 \times 511{,}000 \times 1.0} \approx 0.0381$ nm², so $(\Delta x)_0 \approx 0.195$ nm, about the size of an atom. For $n = 3$, multiply by $\sqrt7$: $(\Delta x)_3 \approx 0.516$ nm.
</Step>
<Step title="(c) Half and half">
$\langle\frac12m\omega^2\hat x^2\rangle = \frac12m\omega^2\cdot\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\left(n + \frac12\right) = \frac12\hbar\omega\left(n + \frac12\right) = \frac12E_n$. The kinetic energy takes the other half, as for the time averages of a classical oscillator.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Summary

- $\hat H = \hbar\omega(\hat a^\dagger\hat a + \frac12)$ with $[\hat a, \hat a^\dagger] = 1$; the factorized form gives $E \ge \frac12\hbar\omega$ and the ground state from the first-order equation $\hat a\psi_0 = 0$, a Gaussian.
- $\hat N = \hat a^\dagger\hat a$ counts: $\psi_n = (\hat a^\dagger)^n\psi_0/\sqrt{n!}$ has $N = n$ and $E_n = \hbar\omega(n + \frac12)$.
- $\hat a\psi_n = \sqrt n\,\psi_{n-1}$ and $\hat a^\dagger\psi_n = \sqrt{n + 1}\,\psi_{n+1}$.
- $(\Delta x)_n^2 = \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}(n + \frac12)$, found by algebra alone; the ground state has the minimum uncertainty product $\hbar/2$.
