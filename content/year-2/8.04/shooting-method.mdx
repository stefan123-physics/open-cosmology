Few potentials can be solved in closed form. The **shooting method** finds bound states of any one-dimensional potential numerically, using nothing more than the behaviour seen on the previous page: at the wrong energy, the solution runs away (notes pp. 11–12). (MIT's material for this lecture is the last section of Zwiebach's lecture notes, Chapter 12; this page follows it in its own words and adds its own example, a finite square well whose shooting can be followed exactly in a graph. The notes' figure is not reproduced.)

## Setting up the shot

The equation to integrate is (notes p. 11)

<Formula label="The equation to integrate" tex="\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\left(E - V(x)\right)\psi = 0" cite="mit" at="notes p. 11, eq. (4.1)" />

A second-order equation needs two starting values. (The notes describe the even case first, then the search over energies, and only then the odd case and hard walls; this page gathers all the starting values first.) The method is simplest for an even potential, whose bound states are even or odd, integrating from $x = 0$ outwards (notes p. 11).

**Even states.** The equation does not fix the normalization, so choose $\psi(0) = 1$. The slope must vanish, since an even function with a nonzero slope at the origin would have a kink there (notes p. 11):

<Formula label="Starting values for even states" tex="\psi(0) = 1, \qquad \psi'(0) = 0" cite="mit" at="notes p. 11, eqs. (4.2)–(4.3)" />

**Odd states.** An odd function must pass through zero, and the slope is then free up to normalization (notes p. 12):

<Formula label="Starting values for odd states" tex="\psi(0) = 0, \qquad \psi'(0) = 1" cite="mit" at="notes p. 12, eq. (4.4)" />

**Hard walls** (notes p. 12). For a potential that is not even but has a hard wall, start at the wall with $\psi = 0$ and $\psi' = 1$. With two hard walls, start at one and require the solution to reach exactly zero at the other.

## Aiming

Now pick an energy $E_0$ and integrate (notes p. 11). An arbitrary energy is not a bound-state energy, and what typically goes wrong is that the solution cannot be normalized: beyond some point it diverges, say towards $+\infty$. Try other energies until one, $E_1$, gives a divergence towards $-\infty$. Somewhere between $E_0$ and $E_1$ the divergence must switch direction, and there the solution neither rises nor falls but decays: a normalizable bound state. Narrow the interval, keeping one end diverging up and the other down. The divergences then set in farther and farther out, and the interval closes in on the bound-state energy (notes pp. 11–12).

**A practical note** (notes p. 12). Before a computer can integrate, the units must be cleaned out of the equation: replace $x$ by a unit-free variable $u$, with $x = bu$ and $b$ a length built from the constants of the problem (the page's own example below uses the half-width of the well).

## Shooting into a square well

The page's own example: the finite square well of half-width $a$ and strength $z_0^2 = 2mV_0a^2/\hbar^2 = 10.5$ (close to the 10 eV electron well on the square-well page). With $u = x/a$ and $\xi = ka$, $\eta = \kappa a$ as in the Lecture 11 notes (Chapter 11, pp. 7–8, eqs. (2.26)–(2.27)), the equation is $d^2\psi/du^2 = -\xi^2\psi$ inside ($u < 1$) and $d^2\psi/du^2 = \eta^2\psi$ outside, with $\eta^2 = 10.5 - \xi^2$; choosing the energy means choosing $\xi$. Shooting for even states from $\psi(0) = 1$, $\psi'(0) = 0$ gives $\cos\xi u$ inside. Outside, the solution that joins it smoothly at $u = 1$ is

<Formula label="The shot outside the well" tex="\psi(u) = Be^{\eta(u - 1)} + Ce^{-\eta(u - 1)}, \qquad B = \frac12\left(\cos\xi - \frac{\xi}{\eta}\sin\xi\right), \quad C = \frac12\left(\cos\xi + \frac{\xi}{\eta}\sin\xi\right)" cite="notes11" at="p. 8, eq. (2.32): the matching conditions; the growing term and the coefficients B and C are worked out here" />

(found by matching $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at the wall, as in the square-well lecture, but keeping the growing exponential that a bound state must lose; this extension is the page's own). The growing term $Be^{\eta(u - 1)}$ is the runaway. When $B > 0$ the shot goes up, when $B < 0$ it goes down, and $B = 0$ is exactly the bound-state condition $\eta = \xi\tan\xi$ (Chapter 11, eq. (2.34)).

<WorkedExample title="Bracketing the ground state">
<Problem>
For the well above, (a) evaluate $B$ for $\xi = 1.15$ and $\xi = 1.25$ and say which way each shot runs. (b) Narrowing the bracket converges to $\xi \approx 1.1936$; find the energy as a fraction of the depth. (c) What would the first odd shot need instead?
</Problem>
<Step title="(a) Up and down">
For $\xi = 1.15$, $\eta = \sqrt{10.5 - 1.3225} \approx 3.029$ and $B = \frac12\left(\cos 1.15 - \frac{1.15}{3.029}\sin 1.15\right) \approx +0.031$: the shot runs up. For $\xi = 1.25$, $B \approx -0.041$: it runs down. A bound state lies in between.
</Step>
<Step title="(b) The energy">
$|E|/V_0 = \eta^2/z_0^2 = (10.5 - 1.1936^2)/10.5 \approx 0.864$: the ground state sits 86.4% of the way down the well, matching the square-well page's $-8.64$ eV for a 10 eV well.
</Step>
<Step title="(c) Odd shots">
Start from $\psi(0) = 0$, $\psi'(0) = 1$, so $\psi = \sin(\xi u)/\xi$ inside; the runaway changes sign where $\eta = -\xi\cot\xi$, at $\xi \approx 2.336$, the first excited state.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Summary

- Shooting: fix $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at a starting point, choose an energy, integrate outwards.
- Even states start from $\psi(0) = 1$, $\psi'(0) = 0$; odd states from $\psi(0) = 0$, $\psi'(0) = 1$; at a hard wall, $\psi = 0$ and $\psi' = 1$.
- At wrong energies the solution diverges up or down; a bound state lies where the direction flips, and narrowing the bracket finds it.
- Clean the units out first, writing $x = bu$ with a natural length $b$.
