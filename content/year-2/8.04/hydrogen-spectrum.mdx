## Quantum numbers and degeneracy

The radial polynomial has degree $N = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ and the angular momentum number is $l = 0, 1, 2, \dots$; together they give the **principal quantum number** (notes p. 7)

<Formula label="Principal quantum number" tex="n \equiv N + l + 1 = \frac{1}{2\kappa}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots" cite="mit" at="notes p. 7, eq. (2.28)" />

For a given $n$, both $l$ and $N$ lie between $0$ and $n - 1$, and fixing $n$ and $l$ fixes $N$ (notes p. 7, eq. (2.29)). So the independent labels are $n$ and $l$, plus $m$, and the energy (notes p. 7)

<Formula label="Energy levels" tex="E_n = -\frac{Z^2e^2}{2a_0}\,\frac{1}{n^2}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 7, eq. (2.31)" />

depends on $n$ alone. For $n > 1$ several values of $l$ share each energy, and each $l$ brings $2l + 1$ values of $m$: the spectrum is **highly degenerate** (notes p. 8). Every point with integers $N \ge 0$ and $l \ge 0$ is a state, and states of equal $n$ lie along diagonals $N + l = n - 1$ (notes p. 8). The meaning of each label: $n$ fixes the energy, $\hbar^2l(l + 1)$ is the eigenvalue of $\hat L^2$, and $\hbar m$ that of $\hat L_z$ (notes p. 8). Counting (notes p. 8):

| $n$ | $l$ | $m$ | states |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 2 | 0, 1 | 0; −1, 0, 1 | 1 + 3 = 4 |
| 3 | 0, 1, 2 | 0; −1 to 1; −2 to 2 | 1 + 3 + 5 = 9 |

and in general (notes p. 8)

<Formula label="Number of states in a level" tex="\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(2l + 1\right) = \frac{2(n - 1)n}{2} + n = n^2" cite="mit" at="notes p. 8, eq. (2.32)" />

In the usual diagram, with one column for each $l$ and energies $\propto -1/n^2$, $N$ grows up each column, and $N$ counts the **nodes** of the radial function (notes p. 9).

## The wavefunctions

With $\rho = 2Z\kappa r/a_0$ (from the previous page) and $\kappa = 1/(2n)$, the radial variable is (notes p. 9)

<Formula label="Radial variable" tex="\rho = \frac{Zr}{na_0}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 9, eq. (2.33)" />

and the eigenstates, labelled by $(n, l, m)$, are (notes p. 10)

<Formula label="Hydrogen eigenstates" tex="\psi_{nlm}(r, \theta, \phi) = C\left(\frac{r}{a_0}\right)^l\times\left(\text{polynomial in } \frac{r}{a_0} \text{ of degree } N = n - l - 1\right)\times e^{-\frac{Zr}{na_0}}\,Y_{lm}(\theta, \phi)" cite="mit" at="notes p. 10, eqs. (2.34)–(2.35)" />

with $C$ a normalization constant (the notes use a script N; C avoids a clash with the degree $N$). The **ground state** of hydrogen ($Z = 1$), $(n, l, m) = (1, 0, 0)$, has no angular momentum and so no angular dependence (notes p. 10):

<Formula label="Hydrogen ground state" tex="\psi_{100}(r, \theta, \phi) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a_0^3}}\,e^{-r/a_0}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 10, eq. (2.36)" />

<Callout kind="cosmology" title="Hydrogen's lines across the universe">
The energy differences $13.6\ \text{eV}\left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right)$ give hydrogen's spectral lines, recognizable in the light of distant galaxies and gas clouds. Their observed wavelengths are stretched by the expansion of the universe, and the stretch is the redshift $z$, which [8.286 explains](/courses/8-286/cosmological-redshift). The same 13.6 eV binding energy sets the scale for when the cooling early universe turned neutral, at about 4,000 K.
</Callout>

<WorkedExample title="Counting states and reading lines">
<Problem>
(a) List the states with $n = 4$ and check that there are $n^2$. (b) Find the energy and wavelength of the photon emitted in the $n = 2 \to 1$ transition ($13.606$ eV, $hc = 1239.8$ eV nm). (c) Find the most probable distance and the average distance $\langle r\rangle$ of the electron from the proton in the ground state.
</Problem>
<Step title="(a) Sixteen states">
$l = 0, 1, 2, 3$ give $1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16 = 4^2$ states, with radial polynomials of degree $N = 3, 2, 1, 0$.
</Step>
<Step title="(b) Lyman alpha">
$\Delta E = 13.606\left(1 - \frac14\right) \approx 10.20$ eV, so $\lambda = 1239.8/10.20 \approx 121.5$ nm, in the ultraviolet. (The measured line is at 121.6 nm: the reduced mass makes the binding slightly weaker, by the factor $\mu/m_e$ of the previous page.) A galaxy at redshift $z = 3$ shows this line at $4 \times 121.5 \approx 486$ nm, in visible light.
</Step>
<Step title="(c) Distances">
The radial probability density is $4r^2e^{-2r/a_0}/a_0^3$, whose derivative vanishes at $r = a_0$: the Bohr radius is the most probable distance. The average is $\langle r\rangle = \frac{4}{a_0^3}\int_0^\infty r^3e^{-2r/a_0}dr = \frac{4}{a_0^3}\cdot\frac{6a_0^4}{16} = \frac32a_0$, larger, because of the long tail.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Summary

- Hydrogen states carry $(n, l, m)$ with $n = N + l + 1$, $0 \le l \le n - 1$, $-l \le m \le l$; $N$ is the number of radial nodes.
- The energy $E_n = -\frac{Z^2e^2}{2a_0n^2}$ depends only on $n$, so each level holds $n^2$ states (ignoring spin).
- $\psi_{nlm} \propto (r/a_0)^l \times (\text{polynomial of degree } n - l - 1) \times e^{-Zr/(na_0)}\,Y_{lm}$; the ground state is $e^{-r/a_0}/\sqrt{\pi a_0^3}$.
- Transitions between levels give hydrogen's spectral lines, such as Lyman alpha near 122 nm.
