So far the energy eigenstates studied have been bound states. **Scattering states** are energy eigenstates that cannot be normalized, like momentum eigenstates; they are not states of a single particle, but superposing them builds wave packets that describe a particle being scattered (notes p. 1). This lecture works through a potential step (notes pp. 1–10). (MIT's material for this lecture is Zwiebach's lecture notes, Chapter 16; this page follows their order in its own words, with its own worked example. The notes' figures are not reproduced.)

## Energy above the step

Look for stationary states $\Psi = \psi(x)e^{-iEt/\hbar}$ with $E > V_0$, so the whole line is classically allowed (notes p. 2). Picture a wave coming in from the left: it should produce a reflected wave on the left and a transmitted wave on the right, so (notes p. 2)

<Formula label="Incident, reflected and transmitted waves" tex="\psi(x) = \begin{cases} Ae^{ikx} + Be^{-ikx}, & x < 0 \\ Ce^{i\bar kx}, & x > 0 \end{cases}, \qquad k^2 = \frac{2mE}{\hbar^2}, \quad \bar k^2 = \frac{2m(E - V_0)}{\hbar^2}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 2, eqs. (2.3)–(2.4)" />

With the time dependence $e^{-iEt/\hbar}$, $e^{ikx}$ moves towards $+x$; so $A$ is the incident amplitude, $B$ the reflected and $C$ the transmitted one. Continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = 0$ gives $A + B = C$ and $A - B = \frac{\bar k}{k}C$ (eqs. (2.5)–(2.6)). Two equations fix $B$ and $C$ in terms of $A$; the overall scale stays free, by linearity (notes pp. 2–3):

<Formula label="Reflected and transmitted amplitudes" tex="\frac BA = \frac{k - \bar k}{k + \bar k}, \qquad \frac CA = \frac{2k}{k + \bar k}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 3, eq. (2.7)" />

At $E = V_0$, $\bar k = 0$, so $B = A$ and $C = 2A$: a cosine on the left joined to a constant on the right (eq. (2.8)).

**Currents** (notes pp. 3–4). With $J = \frac{\hbar}{m}\text{Im}(\psi^*\psi')$, the incident and reflected waves do not interfere in the current:

<Formula label="Currents on each side" tex="J_L = \frac{\hbar k}{m}\left(|A|^2 - |B|^2\right) = J_A - J_B, \qquad J_R = \frac{\hbar\bar k}{m}|C|^2 = J_C" cite="mit" at="notes p. 3, eqs. (2.10)–(2.11)" />

In a stationary state the density does not change, so by $\partial\rho/\partial t + \partial J/\partial x = 0$ the current is the same everywhere: $J_L = J_R$, which (2.7) indeed satisfies (eq. (2.12); the notes' (2.11) and (2.12) have small typos, a stray subscript and a missing $1/m$, corrected here). If $A$ is real, so are $B$ and $C$. So $J_A = J_B + J_C$, and the **reflection** and **transmission coefficients**, ratios of currents, add up to one (notes p. 4):

<Formula label="Reflection and transmission coefficients" tex="R \equiv \frac{J_B}{J_A} = \left(\frac{k - \bar k}{k + \bar k}\right)^2, \qquad T \equiv \frac{J_C}{J_A} = \frac{\bar k}{k}\frac{|C|^2}{|A|^2} = \frac{4k\bar k}{(k + \bar k)^2}, \qquad R + T = 1" cite="mit" at="notes p. 4, eqs. (2.14)–(2.16)" />

Note that $T$ is not $|C|^2/|A|^2$, because the wavenumbers on the two sides differ. At $E = V_0$, $R = 1$ and $T = 0$: the constant on the right carries no current. This also follows by continuity: below the step $T$ must vanish (a decaying exponential carries no current), and $R$ and $T$ change continuously with energy (notes p. 4).

## Energy below the step

Now $E < V_0$ and the right side is forbidden (notes pp. 4–5). There the solution must decay, $Ce^{-\kappa x}$ with $\kappa^2 = 2m(V_0 - E)/\hbar^2$ (eq. (3.18)). No new calculation is needed: the replacement $\bar k \to i\kappa$ turns the old solution into the new one, so (notes p. 5)

<Formula label="Reflection below the step" tex="\frac BA = \frac{k - i\kappa}{k + i\kappa} = -e^{2i\delta(E)}, \qquad \delta(E) = \tan^{-1}\frac{k}{\kappa} = \tan^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{E}{V_0 - E}}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 5, eqs. (3.20)–(3.22)" />

$|B| = |A|$, so $J_B = J_A$, $J_C = 0$: **total reflection**, $R = 1$, $T = 0$. The ratio is a pure phase: $\kappa + ik$ has phase $\delta$ and $\kappa - ik$ phase $-\delta$, and the minus sign is kept outside so that $\delta \to 0$ as $E \to 0$; $\delta$ grows to $\pi/2$ as $E \to V_0$ (notes p. 5). On the left (notes p. 5):

<Formula label="Standing wave on the left" tex="\psi(x) = 2iAe^{i\delta(E)}\sin\left(kx - \delta(E)\right), \qquad |\psi|^2 = 4|A|^2\sin^2\left(kx - \delta(E)\right)" cite="mit" at="notes p. 5, eqs. (3.23)–(3.24)" />

Extrapolated into the forbidden region, this sine would vanish at $x_0 = \delta/k > 0$; the actual density there decays exponentially (notes p. 6). For later use (notes p. 6):

<Formula label="Rate of change of the phase" tex="\delta'(E) \equiv \frac{d\delta}{dE} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{E\left(V_0 - E\right)}}" cite="mit" at="notes p. 6, eq. (3.25)" />

which becomes infinite at both $E \to 0$ and $E \to V_0$.

## Wave packets

Real particles are wave packets (notes pp. 6–8). Superposing the $E > V_0$ solutions (with $A = 1$) with a real weight $f(k)$ peaked at $k_0$, using only $k$ above the threshold where $E = V_0$ and only positive $k$ (genuinely incoming waves), splits the solution into an incident and a reflected packet for $x < 0$ and a transmitted one for $x > 0$ (eqs. (4.26)–(4.30)). Stationary phase locates each peak:

- **Incident**: $\frac{d}{dk}\left(kx - \frac{\hbar k^2t}{2m}\right) = 0$ at $k_0$ gives $x = \frac{\hbar k_0}{m}t$, a peak moving right that exists (in $x < 0$) only for $t < 0$ and reaches the step at $t = 0$ (eq. (4.31)).
- **Reflected**: the phase $-kx - \frac{\hbar k^2t}{2m}$ gives $x = -\frac{\hbar k_0}{m}t$, moving left, present only for $t > 0$ (eq. (4.32)).
- **Transmitted**: since $\bar k\,d\bar k = k\,dk$, the phase $\bar kx - \frac{\hbar k^2t}{2m}$ gives $x = \frac{\hbar\bar k}{m}t$, moving right at the slower speed $\hbar\bar k/m$ for $t > 0$ (eqs. (4.33)–(4.35)).

At large negative times only the incident packet is significant; at large positive times only the reflected and transmitted ones. Near $t = 0$ all three overlap and together describe the collision (notes p. 8).

**A delayed echo** (notes p. 9). For a packet built only from energies below $V_0$, each reflected component carries the extra phase $2\delta(E)$. Stationary phase now gives $2\hbar\delta'(E)\frac{\hbar k_0}{m} - x - \frac{\hbar k_0}{m}t = 0$, so (eqs. (4.36)–(4.39))

<Formula label="Time delay of the reflected packet" tex="x = -\frac{\hbar k_0}{m}\left(t - 2\hbar\,\delta'(E)\right), \qquad \text{time delay} = 2\hbar\,\delta'(E)" cite="mit" at="notes p. 9, eqs. (4.39)–(4.40)" />

The delay is positive, and largest for packets of very low energy or just below $V_0$.

**Seeing a particle in the forbidden region** (notes pp. 9–10). Could one find the particle in $x > 0$ and also know that $E < V_0$? That would mean a negative kinetic energy $-(V_0 - E)$. The wavefunction reaches into the forbidden region only about $1/\kappa$. To be sure the particle is there, its position must be measured with $\Delta x \lesssim 1/\kappa$, which gives it a momentum $\Delta p \gtrsim \hbar\kappa$ and an extra kinetic energy (notes p. 10)

<Formula label="Energy added by locating the particle" tex="\Delta E \sim \frac{(\Delta p)^2}{2m} \gtrsim \frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{2m} = V_0 - E" cite="mit" at="notes p. 10, eqs. (4.42)–(4.44)" />

so the total energy becomes at least $V_0$ (eq. (4.45)). The argument is heuristic, but it suggests that no negative kinetic energy is ever observed.

<WorkedExample title="An electron at a 4 eV step">
<Problem>
An electron meets a step of height $V_0 = 4.0$ eV ($\hbar c = 197.33$ eV nm, $m_ec^2 = 511{,}000$ eV, $\hbar = 6.582 \times 10^{-16}$ eV s). (a) For $E = 5.0$ eV, find $R$ and $T$. (b) For $E = 2.0$ eV, find $\delta$, the penetration depth $1/\kappa$, and the time delay of a reflected packet centred at this energy.
</Problem>
<Step title="(a) Above the step">
$\bar k/k = \sqrt{(E - V_0)/E} = \sqrt{0.2} \approx 0.447$. Then $R = \left(\frac{1 - 0.447}{1 + 0.447}\right)^2 \approx 0.146$ and $T = \frac{4 \times 0.447}{1.447^2} \approx 0.854$; they add up to 1. About one electron in seven bounces back, although classically none would.
</Step>
<Step title="(b) Below the step">
$\delta = \tan^{-1}\sqrt{2/2} = \pi/4$. $\kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m_ec^2(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar c} = \frac{\sqrt{2 \times 511{,}000 \times 2.0}}{197.33} \approx 7.25\ \text{nm}^{-1}$, so $1/\kappa \approx 0.138$ nm. The delay is $2\hbar\delta' = \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{E(V_0 - E)}} = \frac{6.582 \times 10^{-16}}{2.0} \approx 3.3 \times 10^{-16}$ s.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

## Summary

- Scattering states are unnormalizable energy eigenstates; wave packets built from them describe scattering.
- Above a step: $B/A = (k - \bar k)/(k + \bar k)$, $C/A = 2k/(k + \bar k)$; $R = J_B/J_A$ and $T = J_C/J_A$ add up to 1, and some reflection occurs even when a classical particle would pass.
- Below a step: total reflection, $B/A = -e^{2i\delta(E)}$ with $\delta = \tan^{-1}\sqrt{E/(V_0 - E)}$; the wave reaches about $1/\kappa$ into the forbidden region.
- Packets: incident, reflected and transmitted peaks move at $\hbar k_0/m$, $-\hbar k_0/m$ and $\hbar\bar k/m$; below the step the echo is delayed by $2\hbar\,d\delta/dE$.
