The [cycloid solution](/courses/8-286/closed-universe-cycloid) describes a closed universe with the parameter $\alpha$ and the development angle $\theta$. Astronomers cannot measure either. What they can measure is the Hubble rate $H$ and the density relative to critical, $\Omega$. The second half of Lecture 9 translates one description into the other.

## From H and Ω to the cycloid

Write the density as a fraction of the critical density, $\rho = \Omega\rho_c$ with $\rho_c = 3H^2/(8\pi G)$, so $\tfrac{8\pi}{3}G\rho = H^2\Omega$. Then the Friedmann equation in the rescaled variable $\tilde a = a/\sqrt k$ reads $H^2 = H^2\Omega - c^2/\tilde a^2$, which gives the size and then $\alpha$:

<Formula label="Size and α from H and Ω" tex="\tilde a = \frac{c}{|H|\sqrt{\Omega - 1}}, \qquad \alpha = \frac{c\,\Omega}{2|H|\,(\Omega - 1)^{3/2}}" cite="reading1" />

The absolute value $|H|$ is needed because $H$ is negative while the universe contracts. Putting these into $\tilde a = \alpha(1 - \cos\theta)$, almost everything cancels:

<Formula label="Ω and the development angle" tex="\cos\theta = \frac{2 - \Omega}{\Omega}, \qquad \Omega = \frac{2}{1 + \cos\theta}" cite="reading1" />

This has a striking consequence. As $t \to 0$ ($\theta \to 0$), $\Omega \to 1$: **even a closed universe starts out arbitrarily close to the critical density.** $\Omega$ then grows, reaching 2 at $\theta = \pi/2$ and becoming *infinite* at maximum size ($\theta = \pi$). There the expansion stops, $H = 0$, so the critical density $3H^2/(8\pi G)$ is zero while the actual density is not; for that instant the universe is momentarily static. During the collapse the story runs in reverse, and $\Omega$ returns to 1 at the Big Crunch.

## The age formula

Time follows from $ct = \alpha(\theta - \sin\theta)$, with $\sin\theta = \pm 2\sqrt{\Omega - 1}/\Omega$. Guth writes the result with an inverse sine:

<Formula label="Age of a closed universe" tex="t = \frac{\Omega}{2|H|(\Omega - 1)^{3/2}}\left\{\arcsin\!\left(\pm\frac{2\sqrt{\Omega - 1}}{\Omega}\right) \mp \frac{2\sqrt{\Omega - 1}}{\Omega}\right\}" cite="reading2" at="slides 6–7" />

The signs inside and outside the braces are always opposite. Because the inverse sine has many branches, one must pick the right one for the phase of the universe's life. Guth's table on slide 7:

| Quadrant of θ | Phase | Ω | Sign | θ range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | expanding | 1 → 2 | upper | 0 → π/2 |
| 2 | expanding | 2 → ∞ | upper | π/2 → π |
| 3 | contracting | ∞ → 2 | lower | π → 3π/2 |
| 4 | contracting | 2 → 1 | lower | 3π/2 → 2π |

If our universe were closed and matter-dominated, it would be in the first quadrant. (In quadrants 2–4, "arcsin" must be read as the branch matching the θ range, not the calculator's principal value. Equivalently, while the universe expands, $\theta = \arccos\big((2 - \Omega)/\Omega\big)$ picks the right angle automatically; the plot below uses that form.)

<WorkedExample title="A universe with twice the critical density">
<Problem>
Suppose a matter-only universe had $\Omega = 2$ and $H = 67.3$ km/s/Mpc today. Find its age, its total lifetime and how much bigger it will get.
</Problem>
<Step title="Development angle">
$\cos\theta = (2 - 2)/2 = 0$, so $\theta = \pi/2$ (first quadrant, expanding).
</Step>
<Step title="Age">
$\dfrac{\Omega}{2(\Omega - 1)^{3/2}} = 1$, so $Ht = \theta - \sin\theta = \pi/2 - 1 = 0.571$. With $1/H = 14.5$ billion years, $t = 8.3$ billion years, younger than the flat universe's 9.7.
</Step>
<Step title="Lifetime">
$t_\text{total} = 2\pi\alpha/c = \dfrac{2\pi\,\Omega}{2|H|(\Omega - 1)^{3/2}} = 2\pi/H = 91$ billion years from Big Bang to Big Crunch.
</Step>
<Step title="Maximum size">
Now $\tilde a = \alpha(1 - \cos\tfrac{\pi}{2}) = \alpha$; at maximum, $2\alpha$. The universe will only double in size, in agreement with $a_{\max} = \Omega/(\Omega - 1) = 2$ found [earlier](/courses/8-286/open-closed-flat) from the conserved energy.
</Step>
</WorkedExample>

<Callout kind="note" title="The open universe">
The matching calculation for an open universe ($k < 0$) uses hyperbolic functions in place of trigonometric ones. Despite the "last!" in the title of Lecture 9's slides, Guth ends the lecture before reaching it and continues in the next lecture, two days later.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Using $\rho = \Omega\rho_c$, the cycloid parameters become $\tilde a = c/(|H|\sqrt{\Omega - 1})$ and $\alpha = c\Omega/[2|H|(\Omega - 1)^{3/2}]$.
- $\Omega = 2/(1 + \cos\theta)$: a closed universe starts at $\Omega = 1$, reaches $\infty$ at maximum size, and returns to 1 at the Big Crunch.
- The age involves an inverse sine whose branch depends on the phase; for $\Omega = 2$, $t = (\pi/2 - 1)/H \approx 0.571/H$.
- Denser closed universes are younger than a flat one with the same $H$; as $\Omega \to 1$ the age approaches $\tfrac23 H^{-1}$.
