The last section of the chapter (notes pp. 45–51) applies everything so far to one classic system: a **symmetric top** with one point fixed, acted on by a constant force, such as a spinning top with its tip on the table, or a gyroscope on a stand. "Symmetric" means two principal moments are equal, $I_1 = I_2$, with the third, $I_3$, about the symmetry axis, different. The notes solve it twice: without gravity, using [Euler's equations](/courses/8-09/euler-equations-rigid-body), and with gravity, using the Lagrangian in [Euler angles](/courses/8-09/rigid-body-coordinates-euler-angles).

## The set-up

Put the fixed point at the origin. The body axes are $(x, y, z)$, with $z$ the symmetry axis, and the fixed axes are $(x_I, y_I, z_I)$. The Euler angles $(\phi, \theta, \psi)$ now have direct meanings: $\theta$ is the tilt of the symmetry axis from the vertical $z_I$, $\dot\phi$ is the rate at which the axis **precesses** around $z_I$, $\dot\psi$ the rate at which the top **spins** about its own axis, and $\dot\theta$ is the **nutation**, the nodding of the axis towards or away from $z_I$. The centre of mass lies on the symmetry axis, at distance $R$ from the fixed point.

With $I_1 = I_2$, Euler's equations become

<Formula label="Euler's equations for a symmetric top" tex="\begin{aligned} I_1\dot\omega_1 - (I_2 - I_3)\,\omega_2\omega_3 &= \tau_1 \\ I_1\dot\omega_2 - (I_3 - I_1)\,\omega_3\omega_1 &= \tau_2 \\ I_3\dot\omega_3 &= 0 = \tau_3 \end{aligned}" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.74), p. 46" />

The third line holds because the weight acts at the centre of mass, on the $z$ axis, so its torque about the fixed point is perpendicular to $z$: $\tau_3 = 0$. And since $I_1 = I_2$, the product term of the third equation is gone. So **$\omega_3$ is constant**, whatever else happens.

## Without gravity: free precession

First take $\vec\tau = 0$ (no gravity, or the top supported at its centre of mass) and look for motion with no nutation, $\dot\theta = 0$. With no torque, the angular momentum is fixed in space:

<Formula label="Angular momentum is constant" tex="\frac{d\vec L}{dt} = \vec\tau = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \vec L = \text{constant}" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.75), p. 46" />

Define the constant rate

<Formula label="Body-frame precession rate" tex="\Omega \equiv \frac{I_3 - I_1}{I_1}\,\omega_3" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.76), p. 46" />

and the first two Euler equations become a pair of linear equations:

<Formula label="Euler's equations for the free symmetric top" tex="\dot\omega_1 + \Omega\,\omega_2 = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \dot\omega_2 - \Omega\,\omega_1 = 0" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.77), p. 46" />

The simplest solution is $\omega_1 = \omega_2 = 0$: the top just spins about its symmetry axis,

<Formula label="Pure spin" tex="\vec L = L_3\,\vec e_3, \quad L_3 = I_3\omega_3; \qquad \omega_1 = \omega_2 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \dot\theta = \dot\phi = 0 \text{ and } \dot\psi = \omega_3" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.78), p. 46" />

and $\vec L$ is parallel to $\vec\omega$. In general, though, $\vec\omega$ is not along the axis, and (2.77) is solved by

<Formula label="General free solution" tex="\omega_1 = C\sin(\Omega t + D) \quad\text{and}\quad \omega_2 = -C\cos(\Omega t + D)" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.79), p. 46" />

with $C > 0$ (a minus sign can go into the phase $D$). In the body frame, the part of $\vec\omega$ across the symmetry axis has constant length $C$ and turns around the axis at the rate $\Omega$: $\vec\omega$ **precesses about the body $z$ axis**. Its length is constant too, $\omega^2 = C^2 + \omega_3^2$. So is the kinetic energy $T_R = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\vec\omega \cdot \vec L$, and since $\vec L$ is fixed and both $|\vec\omega|$ and $\vec\omega \cdot \vec L$ are constant, $\vec\omega$ also keeps a constant angle to $\vec L$: it precesses about $\vec L$ as well (notes p. 47).

A good picture is two cones. The **body cone**, traced by $\vec\omega$ around the symmetry axis and fixed in the body, rolls without slipping on the **space cone**, traced by $\vec\omega$ around $\vec L$ and fixed in space; the line where they touch is the instantaneous rotation axis $\vec\omega$. For an elongated (prolate) body, $I_1 = I_2 > I_3$, the body cone rolls on the outside of the space cone; for a flattened (oblate) body, $I_3 > I_1$, the body cone surrounds the space cone.

**In Euler angles.** Choose the fixed $z_I$ axis along $\vec L$. With no nutation, $\theta = \theta_0$ is constant, and the Euler-angle expressions for $\omega_1, \omega_2, \omega_3$ from Section 2.4 must reproduce (2.79):

<Formula label="Matching the free solution to Euler angles" tex="\begin{pmatrix} C\sin(\Omega t + D) \\ -C\cos(\Omega t + D) \\ \omega_3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \sin\theta_0\sin\psi\;\dot\phi \\ \sin\theta_0\cos\psi\;\dot\phi \\ \cos\theta_0\,\dot\phi + \dot\psi \end{pmatrix}" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.80), p. 47" />

Squaring and adding the first two rows removes $\psi$:

<Formula label="Precession rate squared" tex="C^2 = \sin^2\theta_0\;\dot\phi^2" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.81), p. 47" />

Taking the positive root,

<Formula label="Steady precession" tex="\dot\phi = \frac{C}{\sin\theta_0} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \phi = \frac{C}{\sin\theta_0}\,t + \phi_0" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.82), p. 47" />

The first two rows then hold with $\psi = \pi - \Omega t - D$, since $\sin\psi = \sin(\Omega t + D)$ and $\cos\psi = -\cos(\Omega t + D)$. So both $\phi$ and $\psi$ change at constant rates, and the third row fixes a relation between the constants:

<Formula label="Relation between the constants" tex="\omega_3 = C\cot\theta_0 - \Omega" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.83), p. 48" />

The other sign of the square root in (2.81) gives the mirror solution:

<Formula label="The other sign" tex="\dot\phi = -\frac{C}{\sin\theta_0} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \psi = -\Omega t - D \quad\text{and}\quad \omega_3 = -C\cot\theta_0 - \Omega" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.84), p. 48" />

Either way, $\dot\phi$ and $\dot\psi$ are constant and $\dot\theta = 0$: the symmetry axis sweeps steadily round $\vec L$ at a fixed angle. A short step beyond the notes gives the precession rate a simple form. Insert $\Omega$ from (2.76) into (2.83): $\omega_3(1 + (I_3 - I_1)/I_1) = C\cot\theta_0$, so $C = I_3\omega_3\tan\theta_0/I_1$, and

<Formula label="Free precession rate in terms of L (this page's own step)" tex="\dot\phi = \frac{C}{\sin\theta_0} = \frac{I_3\,\omega_3}{I_1\cos\theta_0} = \frac{L}{I_1}" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, follows from eqs. (2.76), (2.82), (2.83), pp. 46–48" />

using $L\cos\theta_0 = L_3 = I_3\omega_3$, the component of the fixed $\vec L$ along the symmetry axis. A thrown, wobbling plate or football precesses at $L/I_1$, while in the body frame its wobble turns at $\Omega$.

## With gravity: the Lagrangian

Now let gravity act, so $\vec\tau \ne 0$, and allow nutation, $\dot\theta \ne 0$. The Euler equations are no longer convenient, because the torque depends on the orientation. The notes switch to the Lagrangian. Because $I_1 = I_2$, the kinetic energy needs only $\omega_1^2 + \omega_2^2$, and with $\vec\omega$ in Euler angles,

<Formula label="Kinetic energy and ω in Euler angles" tex="T = \frac{1}{2}\left(I_1(\omega_1^2 + \omega_2^2) + I_3\,\omega_3^2\right), \qquad \vec\omega = \begin{pmatrix} \sin\theta\sin\psi\,\dot\phi + \cos\psi\,\dot\theta \\ \sin\theta\cos\psi\,\dot\phi - \sin\psi\,\dot\theta \\ \cos\theta\,\dot\phi + \dot\psi \end{pmatrix}" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.85), p. 48" />

the $\psi$-dependence drops out of $\omega_1^2 + \omega_2^2 = \dot\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi^2$:

<Formula label="Kinetic energy of the symmetric top" tex="T = \frac{I_1}{2}\left(\dot\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi^2\right) + \frac{I_3}{2}\left(\dot\psi + \cos\theta\,\dot\phi\right)^2" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.86), p. 48" />

The potential energy is that of the centre of mass at height $R\cos\theta$, $V = mgR\cos\theta$. In $L = T - V$ neither $\phi$ nor $\psi$ appears, only their rates: both are **cyclic**, so their momenta are conserved:

<Formula label="Conserved momentum conjugate to φ" tex="p_\phi = \frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot\phi} = \left[I_1\sin^2\theta + I_3\cos^2\theta\right]\dot\phi + I_3\cos\theta\,\dot\psi" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.87), p. 48" />

<Formula label="Conserved momentum conjugate to ψ" tex="p_\psi = \frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot\psi} = I_3\left(\dot\psi + \cos\theta\,\dot\phi\right) = I_3\,\omega_3" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.88), p. 48" />

$p_\psi$ is the $L_3$ of the free case, now seen as a conservation law. Physically, gravity's torque points along the line of nodes, horizontal and perpendicular to the symmetry axis, so the two components of $\vec L$ perpendicular to it are unchanged: $p_\phi$ is $\vec L$ along the vertical $\hat z_I$, $p_\psi$ is $\vec L$ along the symmetry axis $\hat z$. Since the Lagrangian does not depend on time, the energy is conserved too:

<Formula label="Energy of the heavy top" tex="E = T + V = \frac{I_1}{2}\left(\dot\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi^2\right) + \frac{I_3}{2}\left(\dot\psi + \cos\theta\,\dot\phi\right)^2 + mgR\cos\theta" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.89), p. 48" />

Solve (2.87) and (2.88) for the two rates:

<Formula label="Precession and spin rates" tex="\dot\phi = \frac{p_\phi - p_\psi\cos\theta}{I_1\sin^2\theta}, \qquad \dot\psi = \frac{p_\psi}{I_3} - \frac{(p_\phi - p_\psi\cos\theta)\cos\theta}{I_1\sin^2\theta}" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.90), p. 48" />

Once $\theta(t)$ is known, these give $\phi(t)$ and $\psi(t)$ by integration. To find $\theta(t)$, insert $\dot\phi$ into the energy; $\psi$ and $\phi$ disappear and one equation for $\theta$ alone remains:

<Formula label="Energy as an equation for θ" tex="E = \frac{I_1}{2}\dot\theta^2 + \frac{(p_\phi - p_\psi\cos\theta)^2}{2I_1\sin^2\theta} + \frac{p_\psi^2}{2I_3} + mgR\cos\theta" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.91), p. 49" />

## The effective potential

This non-linear equation becomes simpler in the variable $u = \cos\theta$:

<Formula label="Change of variable" tex="1 - u^2 = \sin^2\theta, \qquad \dot u = -\sin\theta\,\dot\theta, \qquad \dot\theta^2 = \frac{\dot u^2}{1 - u^2}" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.92), p. 49" />

Multiplying (2.91) through by $(1 - u^2)/I_1$:

<Formula label="Equation for u" tex="\frac{\dot u^2}{2} = \left(\frac{2EI_3 - p_\psi^2}{2I_1I_3} - \frac{mgR}{I_1}\,u\right)(1 - u^2) - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p_\phi - p_\psi u}{I_1}\right)^2 \equiv -V_\text{eff}(u)" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.93), p. 49" />

which defines $V_\text{eff}(u)$, a **cubic** polynomial in $u$. Formally the solution is

<Formula label="Formal solution" tex="dt = \pm\frac{du}{\sqrt{-2V_\text{eff}(u)}}" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.94), p. 49" />

whose integral is an elliptic function, exact but not very illuminating. It is more useful to read (2.93) the other way round:

<Formula label="A particle in the effective potential" tex="\frac{1}{2}\dot u^2 + V_\text{eff}(u) = 0" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.95), p. 50" />

This is the energy equation of a particle of unit mass, at position $u$, moving in the potential $V_\text{eff}(u)$ with total energy zero. The kinetic energy $\tfrac{1}{2}\dot u^2$ is never negative, so the motion is confined to where $V_\text{eff}(u) \le 0$, and the roots of $V_\text{eff}$ are **turning points**. A cubic has up to three roots; physical motion also needs $-1 \le u \le 1$. At $u = \pm 1$ the potential is $\tfrac{1}{2}(p_\phi \mp p_\psi)^2/I_1^2 \ge 0$, and for large $u$ it falls to $-\infty$, so one root lies at or above $u = 1$ (exactly at 1 only when $p_\phi = p_\psi$) and plays no part in the motion. The other two, $u_1 < u_2$ (tilts $\theta_1 > \theta_2$), bracket the region where $V_\text{eff} < 0$, and the top's axis **nutates**, nodding periodically between $\theta_2$ and $\theta_1$ (notes p. 50).

Meanwhile it precesses at $\dot\phi = (p_\phi - p_\psi u)/[I_1(1 - u^2)]$, whose sign can change only where $u$ passes $p_\phi/p_\psi$. That gives four kinds of motion (the notes' Figs. 2.8–2.11, pp. 50–51), traced by the tip of the symmetry axis on a sphere:

- **Wavy precession**: $\dot\phi > 0$ throughout; the axis goes round steadily while nodding up and down.
- **Loops**: $\dot\phi$ has both signs during a nutation; the axis moves backwards for part of each cycle and traces loops.
- **Cusps**: $\dot\phi = 0$ exactly at the upper turning point $\theta_2$, where $\dot\theta = 0$ too; the axis momentarily stops, then falls and picks up forward precession again. This is the motion of a top **released** at tilt $\theta_2$ spinning, but with no precession and no nutation at the start.
- **Steady precession**: $\theta_1 = \theta_2$, a double root; there is no nutation at all.

<WorkedExample title="A toy top released at 30°">
<Problem>
A toy top of mass $m = 0.1$ kg has its centre of mass $R = 4$ cm above the tip, $I_1 = 1.8 \times 10^{-4}$ kg m² about a horizontal axis through the tip, and $I_3 = 4 \times 10^{-5}$ kg m². It is set spinning at $\omega_3 = 300$ s⁻¹ (about 48 turns per second) and released with its axis at $\theta = 30^\circ$ from the vertical, with $\dot\theta = \dot\phi = 0$. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s². Between which tilts does it nutate, and how fast does it precess?
</Problem>
<Step title="The constants">
$mgR = 0.1 \times 9.8 \times 0.04 = 0.0392$ J and $p_\psi = I_3\omega_3 = 0.012$ kg m²/s. Released with $\dot\phi = 0$ at $u_0 = \cos 30^\circ = 0.866$, (2.90) gives $p_\phi = p_\psi u_0$, and the energy is $E = p_\psi^2/(2I_3) + mgR\,u_0$.
</Step>
<Step title="The effective potential">
Insert these into (2.93). The bracket becomes $(mgR/I_1)(u_0 - u)$, so
$V_\text{eff}(u) = (u_0 - u)\left[\tfrac{1}{2}(p_\psi/I_1)^2(u_0 - u) - (mgR/I_1)(1 - u^2)\right]$.
One root is $u_0$ itself, the release point, which is the upper turning point $u_2$. The other comes from $a(1 - u^2) = u_0 - u$ with $a = 2mgRI_1/p_\psi^2 = 0.098$: $u_1 = 0.837$, a tilt of $\theta_1 = 33.2^\circ$.
</Step>
<Step title="The motion">
So the axis nods between $30^\circ$ and $33.2^\circ$: it dips a little as it starts to precess. At the bottom, $\dot\phi = p_\psi(u_0 - u_1)/[I_1(1 - u_1^2)]$, which by the root condition is exactly $2mgR/p_\psi = 6.53$ rad/s; at the top it is $0$. This is the cusped motion. Because the top is fast, the nodding is small and quick, and the precession averages roughly $mgR/p_\psi = 3.27$ rad/s, by that estimate one turn in about 1.9 s (the fast-top estimate; for this top the true average is a few per cent higher): the familiar slow, steady-looking precession of a spinning top.
</Step>

## Summary

- A symmetric top, $I_1 = I_2 \ne I_3$, with a fixed point on its axis has $\omega_3$ constant: gravity exerts no torque about the symmetry axis, and the product term vanishes.
- **Torque-free**: $\omega_1 = C\sin(\Omega t + D)$, $\omega_2 = -C\cos(\Omega t + D)$ with $\Omega = (I_3 - I_1)\omega_3/I_1$, so $\vec\omega$ circles the symmetry axis in the body; from outside, with $z_I$ along the fixed $\vec L$, $\theta = \theta_0$, $\dot\phi = C/\sin\theta_0$ (which equals $L/I_1$) and $\omega_3 = C\cot\theta_0 - \Omega$. The body cone rolls on the space cone.
- **With gravity**: in $L = T - V$ with $T = \tfrac{I_1}{2}(\dot\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi^2) + \tfrac{I_3}{2}(\dot\psi + \cos\theta\,\dot\phi)^2$ and $V = mgR\cos\theta$, the angles $\phi$ and $\psi$ are cyclic, so $p_\phi$, $p_\psi = I_3\omega_3$ and $E$ are conserved.
- With $u = \cos\theta$, $\tfrac{1}{2}\dot u^2 + V_\text{eff}(u) = 0$ with a cubic $V_\text{eff}$: the tilt nutates between two roots $u_1$ and $u_2$, and the sign of $\dot\phi = (p_\phi - p_\psi u)/[I_1(1 - u^2)]$ distinguishes wavy precession, loops, cusps (a released top) and steady precession.
