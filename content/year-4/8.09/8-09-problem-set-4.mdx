## How to answer

Work each problem on paper first. Then type your result in the answer box and press Check. A number box accepts any value within the tolerance written next to it, in the unit named in the question. A formula box takes plain typing, for example `m*g*R*sin(th)`, and accepts every correct way of writing the answer. It treats its letters as independent, so each question says which letters to type: `th` for $\theta$, `psi` for $\psi$, `W` for $\Omega$, `wp` for $\omega'$, `l1` and `l2` for $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$, and so on.

Give a number answer to 3 significant figures and keep 4 in intermediate steps; an answer with only 2 significant figures may or may not be accepted. Where a box needs more digits, its label says so.

MIT gives the points of each problem (10, 16, 14 and 20, together 60). Their split between the parts is ours. Several parts ask for more than their boxes check, such as a derivation or a sketch, and Problem 2(c) has no box at all: open the solution and mark yourself. If you are stuck, open the hints one at a time. Each part ends with its full solution. Try before you open it: an answer given after its solution was opened earns no points. Your score is kept in this browser only.

The conventions are those of the lessons. An overdot is $d/dt$. The Euler angles $(\phi, \theta, \psi)$ are those of the notes: $\phi$ turns about the fixed vertical $z_I$, $\theta$ tilts the body axis $z$ away from $z_I$, and $\psi$ turns about $z$. For small oscillations the displacements $\eta_i$ from equilibrium obey $L = \frac12\left(T_{ij}\dot\eta_i\dot\eta_j - V_{ij}\eta_i\eta_j\right)$, with $V_{ij}$ the second derivatives of the potential at the minimum, and the normal-mode frequencies solve $\det(\hat V - \omega^2\hat T) = 0$. The acceleration of free fall is $g$.

## Problem 1

<Exercise id="p1" title="A heavy symmetric top" points={10} mit="Problem 1">

A heavy symmetric top, $I_1 = I_2$, has one point of its symmetry axis fixed. Its mass is $m$ and its centre of mass lies on the axis, a distance $R$ from the fixed point. $I_1$ and $I_3$ are its principal moments about the fixed point. The top precesses steadily: its axis turns about the fixed vertical axis $z_I$ at a constant angular velocity $\Omega = \dot\phi$, at a constant tilt, $\dot\theta = 0$. Write $\omega' \equiv \dot\psi$ for the rate of spin.

<Part label="a" points={3}>

Find the components of the torque of gravity about the fixed point, in terms of the Euler angles.

The two boxes are our additions. Give the components $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$ along the body axes $x$ and $y$, the first in the first box and the second in the second; type `m`, `g`, `R`, `th` for $\theta$ and `psi` for $\psi$.

<Hint>

The torque is $\vec\tau = R\,\hat z \times (-mg\,\hat z_I)$, with $\hat z$ the symmetry axis. You need $\hat z_I$ in body components.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Read the body components of $\hat z_I$ off the formula for $\vec\omega$ in Euler angles: $\vec\omega = \dot\phi\,\hat z_I + \dot\theta\,\hat e_N + \dot\psi\,\hat z$, where $\hat e_N$ is the line of nodes.

</Hint>

  ]} />

<Answer id="p1a-tau2" label="$\tau_2 =$" expr="-m*g*R*sin(th)*sin(psi)"
  vars={{ m: [0.5, 2], g: [9, 10], R: [0.5, 2], th: [0.9, 1.4], psi: [0.9, 1.4] }} names={{ th: "\\theta", psi: "\\psi" }}
  mistakes={[
    { expr: "m*g*R*sin(th)*sin(psi)", hint: "Check the sign: the line of nodes has body components (cos ψ, −sin ψ, 0), so τ2 is negative for ψ between 0 and π/2." },
    { expr: "-m*g*R*sin(th)*cos(psi)", hint: "The y component of the line of nodes is −sin ψ, not −cos ψ." }
  ]} />

<Solution>

**The idea.** Gravity acts at the centre of mass, $R\hat z$ from the fixed point, with force $-mg\,\hat z_I$. The torque is their cross product, and it lies along the line of nodes.

<Step title="The fixed vertical in body components">

The angular velocity in Euler angles is, in body components,

<Formula label="Angular velocity in Euler angles, body components" tex="\omega_1 = \dot\phi\sin\theta\sin\psi + \dot\theta\cos\psi, \qquad \omega_2 = \dot\phi\sin\theta\cos\psi - \dot\theta\sin\psi, \qquad \omega_3 = \dot\phi\cos\theta + \dot\psi" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.4, eq. (2.70), p. 45" />

Since $\vec\omega = \dot\phi\,\hat z_I + \dot\theta\,\hat e_N + \dot\psi\,\hat z$, the coefficients of $\dot\phi$ are the body components of $\hat z_I = (\sin\theta\sin\psi, \sin\theta\cos\psi, \cos\theta)$, and those of $\dot\theta$ the line of nodes, $\hat e_N = (\cos\psi, -\sin\psi, 0)$.

</Step>

<Step title="The torque">

Crossing $\hat z$ into $\hat z_I$ and multiplying by $-mgR$:

<Formula label="Problem 1(a): the torque of gravity on the top" tex="\vec\tau = -mgR\,\hat z \times \hat z_I = mgR\sin\theta\,\hat e_N, \qquad \tau_1 = mgR\sin\theta\cos\psi, \quad \tau_2 = -mgR\sin\theta\sin\psi, \quad \tau_3 = 0" cite="mit" at="Problem 1(a), p. 2 (our own solution)" />

In the fixed axes the same vector is $mgR\sin\theta\,(\cos\phi, \sin\phi, 0)$: horizontal, along the line of nodes, and it turns with the axis as the top precesses.

</Step>

<Step title="Check the result">

The size $mgR\sin\theta$ is weight times the horizontal lever arm $R\sin\theta$, and it vanishes for an upright top. $\tau_3 = 0$: gravity has no torque about the symmetry axis, as the notes use. The torque is perpendicular to the vertical, so it never changes $L_z$, the momentum $p_\phi$.

</Step>

</Solution>

</Part>

<Part label="b" points={2}>

Write the components of the angular velocity in terms of the Euler angles, for this motion. Explain why $\omega'$ is constant in time.

The two boxes are our additions. In the first, give the component $\omega_3$ along the symmetry axis; type `W` for $\Omega$, `th` and `wp` for $\omega'$. In the second, choose the reason.

<Hint>

Put $\dot\phi = \Omega$ and $\dot\theta = 0$ into the Euler-angle formula for $\vec\omega$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

What does the third of Euler's equations say for a top with $I_1 = I_2$? Then use that $\theta$ and $\Omega$ are fixed.

</Hint>

<Answer id="p1b-w3" label="$\omega_3 =$" expr="W*cos(th) + wp"
  vars={{ W: [0.5, 2], th: [0.3, 1.3], wp: [5, 20] }} names={{ W: "\\Omega", th: "\\theta", wp: "\\omega'" }}
  mistakes={[
    { expr: "wp", hint: "ψ̇ is the spin relative to the turning line of nodes. The precession about z_I adds its own part along the symmetry axis, Ω cos θ." },
    { expr: "W + wp", hint: "Only the part of the precession along the symmetry axis counts: ẑ_I makes the angle θ with it, so the precession adds Ω cos θ, not Ω." }
  ]} />

<Answer id="p1b-why" label="ω′ stays constant because:" choices={["the total torque on the top is zero, so its angular velocity cannot change", "Ω and θ are constant, and ω′ is fixed by them whatever ω3 does", "a top with I3 larger than I1 always spins at a steady rate", "τ3 = 0 and I1 = I2 keep ω3 fixed, so ω′ = ω3 − Ω cos θ is fixed too"]} answer={3} />

<Solution>

**The idea.** In steady precession only $\psi$ and $\phi$ change, at the rates $\omega'$ and $\Omega$. The third Euler equation fixes $\omega_3$; with $\theta$ and $\Omega$ fixed, that fixes $\omega'$ too.

<Step title="The angular velocity">

With $\dot\phi = \Omega$ and $\dot\theta = 0$ in (2.70):

<Formula label="Problem 1(b): the angular velocity in steady precession" tex="\omega_1 = \Omega\sin\theta\sin\psi, \qquad \omega_2 = \Omega\sin\theta\cos\psi, \qquad \omega_3 = \Omega\cos\theta + \omega'" cite="mit" at="Problem 1(b), p. 2 (our own solution)" />

The part across the axis, $\Omega\sin\theta$, turns in the body as $\psi$ advances; the part along it is constant.

</Step>

<Step title="Why the spin is constant">

For a symmetric top the third Euler equation reads

<Formula label="Euler's equations for a symmetric top" tex="I_1\dot\omega_1 - (I_2 - I_3)\,\omega_2\omega_3 = \tau_1, \qquad I_1\dot\omega_2 - (I_3 - I_1)\,\omega_3\omega_1 = \tau_2, \qquad I_3\dot\omega_3 = 0 = \tau_3" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.74), p. 46" />

The product term of the third equation has gone because $I_1 = I_2$, and $\tau_3 = 0$ by (a). So $\omega_3$ is constant, and $\omega' = \omega_3 - \Omega\cos\theta$ is constant because $\Omega$ and $\theta$ are.

</Step>

<Step title="Check the result">

The same follows from the Lagrangian: $\psi$ is cyclic, so $p_\psi = I_3\omega_3$ is conserved. The total torque is not zero (part (a)), so $\vec\omega$ itself changes: its components across the axis turn with $\psi$. $|\vec\omega|^2 = \Omega^2\sin^2\theta + \omega_3^2$ is constant, since $\Omega$, $\theta$ and $\omega_3$ are.

</Step>

</Solution>

</Part>

<Part label="c" points={5}>

Find the condition $\omega'$ must meet for steady precession to be possible: a minimum value. Which kind of top meets it for every possible $\omega'$?

The three boxes are our additions. In the first, take $I_1 > I_3$ and $0 < \theta < \pi/2$, and give the smallest spin $\omega'$ for which steady precession is possible; type `m`, `g`, `R`, `I1`, `I3` and `th`. In the second, take a toy top (our numbers): $m = 0.10$ kg, $R = 4.0$ cm, $I_1 = 1.8 \times 10^{-4}$ kg m², $I_3 = 4.0 \times 10^{-5}$ kg m², $\theta = 30°$ and $g = 9.81$ m/s²; give that smallest spin in rad/s (tolerance 1%). In the third, choose which tops, standing above the fixed point, can precess steadily at any spin.

<Hint>

Steady precession is a solution with $\ddot\theta = \dot\theta = 0$. Write the Lagrangian with $T$ from the notes and $V = mgR\cos\theta$, and set the $\theta$ equation to these values.

</Hint>

<Hint>

After dividing by $\sin\theta$ you get a quadratic equation for $\Omega$. When does it have a real root?

</Hint>

    { expr: "sqrt(m*g*R*(I1 - I3)*cos(th))/I3", hint: "The discriminant of aΩ² + bΩ + c is b² − 4ac: the 4 under the square root gives a factor 2 in front." }

<Answer id="p1c-num" label="Smallest spin of the toy top:" value="109" unit="rad/s" tolerance="1%" calc="p1c-num" />

<Answer id="p1c-tops" label="With the centre of mass above the fixed point, steady precession is possible at any spin for:" choices={["long, thin tops, with I1 larger than I3", "any top, if its axis is tilted close enough to the vertical", "short, wide tops, with I3 larger than I1", "no top: some spin is always needed"]} answer={2} />

<Solution>

**The idea.** Steady precession needs the torque of gravity to be exactly what it takes to turn $\vec L$ at the rate $\Omega$. That is a quadratic equation for $\Omega$; it has real roots only if the spin is large enough, unless the sign of its leading term rules a problem out.

<Step title="The equation for θ">

The kinetic energy of the notes,

<Formula label="Kinetic energy of the symmetric top" tex="T = \frac{I_1}{2}\left(\dot\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi^2\right) + \frac{I_3}{2}\left(\dot\psi + \cos\theta\,\dot\phi\right)^2" cite="reading1" at="Section 2.5, eq. (2.86), p. 48" />

with $V = mgR\cos\theta$, gives the $\theta$ equation $I_1\ddot\theta = I_1\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\dot\phi^2 - I_3\omega_3\sin\theta\,\dot\phi + mgR\sin\theta$. With $\ddot\theta = 0$, $\dot\phi = \Omega$, $\omega_3 = \omega' + \Omega\cos\theta$, and $\sin\theta \ne 0$:

<Formula label="Problem 1(c): the condition for steady precession" tex="(I_1 - I_3)\cos\theta\;\Omega^2 - I_3\,\omega'\,\Omega + mgR = 0" cite="mit" at="Problem 1(c), p. 2 (our own solution)" />

</Step>

<Step title="The minimum spin">

A real $\Omega$ needs a discriminant that is not negative:

<Formula label="Problem 1(c): the minimum condition on the spin" tex="I_3^2\,\omega'^2 \ge 4\,mgR\,(I_1 - I_3)\cos\theta, \qquad \text{so for } (I_1 - I_3)\cos\theta > 0: \quad \omega' \ge \frac{2}{I_3}\sqrt{mgR\,(I_1 - I_3)\cos\theta}" cite="mit" at="Problem 1(c), p. 2 (our own solution)" />

Above the minimum there are two rates, a slow and a fast precession, $\Omega = \left[I_3\omega' \pm \sqrt{I_3^2\omega'^2 - 4mgR(I_1 - I_3)\cos\theta}\right]/\left[2(I_1 - I_3)\cos\theta\right]$; at the minimum they merge.

</Step>

<Step title="Which tops always manage">

The condition holds for every $\omega'$ when $(I_1 - I_3)\cos\theta \le 0$. With the centre of mass above the fixed point ($\cos\theta > 0$) that means $I_3 \ge I_1$: a short, wide top whose moment about its axis is at least that about a horizontal axis through the tip. (For $I_3 = I_1$ the equation is linear, $\Omega = mgR/(I_3\omega')$, so any spin other than zero works.) A top hanging below its fixed point ($\cos\theta < 0$) with $I_1 > I_3$ manages too. A long, thin top above its fixed point always needs some spin; a tilt closer to the vertical makes $\cos\theta$ larger and raises the minimum.

</Step>

<Step title="Our numbers">

$mgR = 0.10 \times 9.81 \times 0.040 = 0.0392$ J and $I_1 - I_3 = 1.4 \times 10^{-4}$ kg m², so the smallest spin is $2\sqrt{0.0392 \times 1.4 \times 10^{-4} \times 0.866}/(4.0 \times 10^{-5}) = 109$ rad/s, about 17 turns per second. At that spin the two rates merge at $\Omega = I_3\omega'/[2(I_1 - I_3)\cos\theta] = 18.0$ rad/s.

</Step>

<Step title="Check the result">

For a fast top, with $\omega'$ far above that minimum, the slow root is $\Omega \approx mgR/(I_3\omega')$, the familiar slow precession: torque $mgR\sin\theta$ turning a spin angular momentum $I_3\omega'$ whose horizontal part is $I_3\omega'\sin\theta$. At $\theta = \pi/2$ the condition is always met and $\Omega = mgR/(I_3\omega')$ exactly: a horizontal gyroscope. The units: $mgR\,I/I_3^2$ is energy per moment of inertia, a rate squared.

</Step>

</Solution>

</Part>

</Exercise>

## Problem 2

<Exercise id="p2" title="Three point masses on a circle" points={16} mit="Problem 2">

Three particles of equal mass $m$ move on a circle of radius $a$. Neighbouring particles push each other apart through the potential

<Formula label="Problem 2: the potential of the three masses" tex="V(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = V_0\left(e^{-2\alpha} + e^{-2\beta} + e^{-2\gamma}\right)" cite="mit" at="Problem 2, p. 2" />

where $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are the angles, in radians, between neighbouring particles, measured at the centre, so $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 2\pi$. In equilibrium the particles are evenly spaced, $\alpha = \beta = \gamma = 2\pi/3$. Let $\theta_1$, $\theta_2$, $\theta_3$ be the angular displacements of the three particles from their equilibrium positions. Our choice, as in MIT's figure up to the sense of turning: going counterclockwise round the circle the particles come in the order 1, 2, 3, every $\theta_i$ is counted counterclockwise, $\alpha$ is the angle from particle 1 to particle 2, $\beta$ from 2 to 3, and $\gamma$ from 3 back to 1.

<Part label="a" points={6}>

For small oscillations about equilibrium, find the normal-mode frequencies and the normalised normal modes.

The two boxes are our additions. In the first, give the largest normal-mode angular frequency; type `V0`, `m` and `a` (`pi` and `exp` are allowed). In the second, choose the list of all three frequencies, with $\omega$ the answer to the first box.

<Hint>

Write $\alpha = 2\pi/3 + \theta_2 - \theta_1$ and similarly for $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Expand each exponential to second order in the $\theta$'s; the first-order terms add up to zero.

</Hint>

<Hint>

The kinetic energy is $\frac12 ma^2\left(\dot\theta_1^2 + \dot\theta_2^2 + \dot\theta_3^2\right)$, so $\hat T$ is a multiple of the unit matrix. The matrix $\hat V$ has a simple pattern: what does it do to $(1, 1, 1)$?

</Hint>

<Answer id="p2a-w" label="Largest $\omega =$" expr="sqrt(12*V0*exp(-4*pi/3)/(m*a^2))"
  vars={{ V0: [0.5, 2], m: [0.5, 2], a: [1.2, 2] }} names={{ V0: "V_0" }}
  mistakes={[
    { expr: "sqrt(12*V0*exp(-4*pi/3)/m)", hint: "The particles move along the circle at speed aθ̇, so the kinetic energy is ½ma²θ̇²: the inertia in each equation is ma², not m." },
    { expr: "sqrt(6*V0*exp(-4*pi/3)/(m*a^2))", hint: "This ω² is half the right one. With V ≈ ½ Σ Vij θiθj, the matrix entries are the second derivatives ∂²V/∂θi∂θj; the diagonal entries are twice the coefficients of the squares in the quadratic form." },
    { expr: "sqrt(12*V0*exp(-2*pi/3)/(m*a^2))", hint: "Each exponential is e^(−2α) at α = 2π/3, which is e^(−4π/3), not e^(−2π/3)." }
  ]} />

<Answer id="p2a-list" label="The three normal-mode frequencies are:" choices={["0, ω and ω", "ω, ω and ω", "0, ω and √3 ω", "ω/√3, ω and √3 ω"]} answer={0} />

<Solution>

**The idea.** Expand the potential to second order about the even spacing. The kinetic matrix is a multiple of the unit matrix, so the problem is an ordinary eigenvalue problem for $\hat V$. One mode is a turn of the whole ring, which costs no energy.

<Step title="The quadratic Lagrangian">

With $\alpha = 2\pi/3 + \theta_2 - \theta_1$, $\beta = 2\pi/3 + \theta_3 - \theta_2$, $\gamma = 2\pi/3 + \theta_1 - \theta_3$ and $e^{-2x} \approx 1 - 2x + 2x^2$, the linear terms cancel because the three differences add up to zero. Write $\kappa = V_0e^{-4\pi/3}$. Then, in the form of the notes,

<Formula label="Lagrangian to quadratic order" tex="L = \frac{1}{2}\left(T_{ij}\dot\eta_i\dot\eta_j - V_{ij}\eta_i\eta_j\right)" cite="reading2" at="Chapter 3 introduction, eq. (3.3), p. 52" />

we have, with $\eta_i = \theta_i$,

<Formula label="Problem 2(a): the matrices of the three masses" tex="\hat T = ma^2\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \hat V = 4\kappa\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \kappa = V_0e^{-4\pi/3}" cite="mit" at="Problem 2(a), p. 2 (our own solution)" />

</Step>

<Step title="The frequencies">

The frequencies solve

<Formula label="The characteristic equation" tex="\det(\hat V - \lambda\hat T) = 0" cite="reading2" at="Section 3.1, eq. (3.9), p. 53" />

with $\lambda = \omega^2$. The pattern matrix has eigenvalue $0$ on $(1, 1, 1)$ and $3$ on every vector whose entries add up to zero, so

<Formula label="Problem 2(a): the normal-mode frequencies" tex="\omega_1 = 0, \qquad \omega_2 = \omega_3 = \omega = \sqrt{\frac{12\,V_0\,e^{-4\pi/3}}{ma^2}}" cite="mit" at="Problem 2(a), p. 2 (our own solution)" />

</Step>

<Step title="The normalised modes">

The notes normalise the modes with the kinetic matrix,

<Formula label="Normalisation with the kinetic matrix" tex="\vec a^{(\beta)\dagger}\cdot\hat T\cdot\vec a^{(\alpha)} = \delta_{\alpha\beta}" cite="reading2" at="Section 3.1, eq. (3.20), p. 55" />

Here that gives

<Formula label="Problem 2(a): normalised normal modes" tex="\vec a^{(1)} = \frac{1}{a\sqrt{3m}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \vec a^{(2)} = \frac{1}{a\sqrt{2m}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \vec a^{(3)} = \frac{1}{a\sqrt{6m}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}" cite="mit" at="Problem 2(a), p. 2 (our own solution)" />

Modes 2 and 3 share the frequency $\omega$, so any two orthonormal vectors with entries adding up to zero would do as well; this pair is one choice.

</Step>

<Step title="Check the result">

$\hat V\vec a^{(2)} = 4\kappa(3, -3, 0) = 12\kappa\,(1, -1, 0)$ and $12\kappa/(ma^2) = \omega^2$. The units: $V_0/(ma^2)$ is energy over a moment of inertia, a rate squared. The zero frequency is no accident: the potential depends only on the differences of the $\theta$'s, so turning all three particles together costs nothing.

</Step>

</Solution>

</Part>

<Part label="b" points={3}>

What are the normal coordinates, and their equations of motion?

The box is our addition. Choose the equation obeyed by the normal coordinate proportional to $\theta_1 + \theta_2 + \theta_3$.

<Hint>

The normal coordinates are $\vec\xi$ with $\vec\theta = A\vec\xi$, where the columns of $A$ are the normalised modes. Since $A^T\hat TA = 1$, $\vec\xi = A^T\hat T\vec\theta$.

</Hint>

<Answer id="p2b-zero" label="That normal coordinate obeys:" choices={["the same equation as the others, with frequency ω", "a free equation: its second derivative is zero", "an equation with frequency ω/√3", "no equation: it is zero at all times"]} answer={1} />

  vars={{ k: [20, 40], m: [0.2, 0.6], g: [9, 10], a: [0.3, 0.8], l: [0.1, 0.4], th0: [0.05, 0.15] }} names={{ l: "\\ell", th0: "\\theta_0" }}

<Solution>

**The idea.** Expand $V$ to second order about equilibrium; the kinetic energy is already quadratic, with constant coefficients. Each spring adds a stiffness $k$ along its line and, because it is under tension, a stiffness (tension)/length across it.

<Step title="The kinetic matrix">

<Formula label="Problem 4(b): the kinetic matrix of the bar" tex="T = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\vec\eta}^T\hat T\dot{\vec\eta}, \qquad \hat T = \begin{pmatrix} m & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & m & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & m\ell^2/3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \vec\eta = (x, y, \phi)" cite="mit" at="Problem 4(b), p. 3 (our own solution)" />

</Step>

<Step title="The potential to second order">

Write $s = \sin\theta_0$, $c = \cos\theta_0$, and $F = mg/(2c)$ for the tension. The unit vector from the left end to its ceiling point is $(-s, c)$, from the right end $(s, c)$. To first order, the left end moves by $(x, y - \ell\phi)$ and the right end by $(x, y + \ell\phi)$; to second order both also move in by $\ell\phi^2/2$. Adding the stretch along each spring, the sideways move across it and the work against the tension:

<Formula label="Problem 4(b): the potential of the bar to second order" tex="V = k\left(sx + c\ell\phi\right)^2 + kc^2y^2 + \frac{F}{a}\left(cx - s\ell\phi\right)^2 + \frac{F}{a}s^2y^2 + Fs\ell\phi^2" cite="mit" at="Problem 4(b), p. 3 (our own solution)" />

To first order in $\theta_0$ ($s \to \theta_0$, $c \to 1$, $F \to mg/2$, $s^2 \to 0$):

<Formula label="Problem 4(b): the potential matrix to first order in the tilt" tex="\hat V = \begin{pmatrix} mg/a & 0 & 2k\ell\theta_0 - mg\ell\theta_0/a \\ 0 & 2k & 0 \\ 2k\ell\theta_0 - mg\ell\theta_0/a & 0 & 2k\ell^2 + mg\ell\theta_0 \end{pmatrix}" cite="mit" at="Problem 4(b), p. 3 (our own solution)" />

</Step>

<Step title="Check the result">

The term $Fs\ell\phi^2$ comes from the linear term of each spring, $F$ times its change in length, applied to the second-order inward move $\ell\phi^2/2$ of the ends; the linear terms in $y$ cancel against $mgy$ because $2Fc = mg$. $V_{11} = mg/a$ is the stiffness of a pendulum of length $a$ and weight $mg$: for small $\theta_0$ the bar swings sideways like one. Its units: $V_{11}$ and $V_{22}$ in N/m, $V_{13}$ in N, $V_{33}$ in J, as the coordinates require.

</Step>

<Answer id="p4c-num" label="Angular frequency of the rocking mode:" value="10.57" unit="rad/s" tolerance="1%" calc="p4c-num" />

<Answer id="p4c-flat" label="With vertical springs, θ0 = 0:" choices={["the vertical bounce would mix with the rocking, so no mode would stay pure", "the sideways swing would lose its restoring force and have frequency zero", "swing and rocking would no longer mix, and rocking would be at √(6k/m)", "nothing would change: the same modes, at the same frequencies"]} answer={2} />

<Solution>

**The idea.** $\hat T$ is diagonal and $y$ is coupled to nothing, so the bounce is a mode on its own. $x$ and $\phi$ are coupled only at first order in $\theta_0$. A coupling of first order changes the frequencies only at second order, provided $g/a$ is not close to $6k/m$ (here 16.4 against 100 s⁻²), so to first order each frequency squared is a diagonal entry of $\hat V$ divided by the same entry of $\hat T$, and the modes mix $x$ and $\phi$ at first order.

<Step title="The three frequencies">

<Formula label="Problem 4(c): the normal-mode frequencies of the bar" tex="\omega_y^2 = \frac{2k}{m}, \qquad \omega_x^2 = \frac{g}{a}, \qquad \omega_\phi^2 = \frac{2k\ell^2 + mg\ell\theta_0}{m\ell^2/3} = \frac{6k}{m} + \frac{3g\theta_0}{\ell}" cite="mit" at="Problem 4(c), p. 3 (our own solution)" />

all to first order in $\theta_0$; the shift caused by $V_{13}$ is of order $\theta_0^2$.

</Step>

<Step title="The modes">

- **Bounce**, $\vec\eta \propto (0, 1, 0)$: the bar moves straight up and down, staying horizontal; both springs stretch and shrink together.
- **Swing**, $\vec\eta \propto (1, 0, \epsilon_x)$: the bar swings sideways like a pendulum hanging from the two springs, tilting slightly as it goes, with $\epsilon_x = -V_{13}/(V_{33} - \omega_x^2 m\ell^2/3)$, of first order in $\theta_0$.
- **Rock**, $\vec\eta \propto (\epsilon_\phi, 0, 1)$: the bar see-saws about its centre, one end up while the other goes down, and its centre slides slightly sideways, with $\epsilon_\phi = -V_{13}/(V_{11} - \omega_\phi^2 m)$, also of first order in $\theta_0$.

Normalised with $\hat T$, as in (3.20), the three vectors are the columns of a matrix $A$ that turns $\hat T$ into the unit matrix and $\hat V$ into the diagonal matrix of the $\omega^2$:

<Formula label="Simultaneous diagonalisation" tex="A^T\hat TA = 1 \quad\text{and}\quad A^T\hat VA = \hat\lambda" cite="reading2" at="Section 3.2, eq. (3.27), p. 56" />

</Step>

<Step title="Our numbers">

$6k/m = 100$ s⁻² and $3g\theta_0/\ell = 11.77$ s⁻², so $\omega_\phi = \sqrt{111.77} = 10.57$ rad/s; the bounce is at $\sqrt{2k/m} = 5.77$ rad/s and the swing at $\sqrt{g/a} = 4.04$ rad/s. In our calculation file the eigenvalues of the full matrices, without the first-order approximation, differ from these by less than 1%.

</Step>

<Step title="If the springs were vertical">

For $\theta_0 = 0$, $V_{13} = 0$: the swing and the rock are pure, the bar swinging without tilting and rocking with its centre fixed, and the rocking frequency drops to $\sqrt{6k/m}$. The swing keeps its frequency $\sqrt{g/a}$ to this order, since its restoring force comes from the tension, not from the tilt.

</Step>

<Step title="Check the result">

The rock without gravity's term, $6k/m$, follows directly: turning by $\phi$ stretches one spring by $\ell\phi$ and squeezes the other by as much, a torque $2k\ell^2\phi$ on a moment of inertia $m\ell^2/3$. The bounce, $2k/m$, is two springs in parallel holding the mass $m$.

</Step>
