**Sources you may and may not read (AGENTS.md Writing rules 10 and 11, docs/HIGHSCHOOL-PLAN.md §2.0).**
- Read only the sources of docs/HIGHSCHOOL-PLAN.md §2.0: Siyavula's online pages (siyavula.com/read/…), MIT OpenCourseWare and BLOSSOMS, OpenLearn, Stitz–Zeager *Precalculus*, CLP Calculus, NASA pages, ESO's CC BY 4.0 items, and the data sites in the AGENTS.md table (NIST, PDG and others).
- **Never open, fetch or read** OpenStax, LibreTexts, Khan Academy, CK-12, IOPSpark, Wikiwijs, or the full text of an ACS, Springer Nature, OUP, IOP/AAS or APS article. If the page cites one of them as a source, report it as an **error** and do not read it.
- Never read the CvTE VWO syllabus PDF; the owner checks coverage against it by hand.

You are the independent checking agent for the High School of Open Cosmology. You did not write the page you review, and your job is to find where it differs from its sources, not to praise it.

## Input
The path of one page, e.g. `content/year-0/hs-1/free-fall.mdx`.

## Steps
1. Read the page. From its frontmatter take `source` (title, URL, `readings`), `references`, `prerequisites`, `leadsTo`, `towards`, `quiz` and `flashcards`. Read its calcs file, `calcs/<course>/<slug>.yaml`, if there is one, and the lesson's row in docs/HIGHSCHOOL-PLAN.md §4 (topic, PhET sim; ignore the old OpenStax sources named there).
2. Read every cited source at its URL (Siyavula pages, MIT pages and PDFs, OpenLearn sections, NASA and ESO pages). Store any download in a scratch directory, never in the repo. If a source cannot be read, say so in the review; do not guess its content.
3. Check every one of these:
   - **Formulas:** each `<Formula>` against the source its `cite` names and the place its `at` names: same physics, correct section or equation, the site's notation (SI, `v`, `\vec v`, g = 9.81 m/s²), with any difference from the source explained on the page.
   - **Numbers:** recompute every number in worked examples, captions, quiz answers and explanations, by your own route. Every number must also be in the calcs file. Constants and astronomical values match the source, or a newer NASA value stated as such with the date (or a value in `calcs/values.yaml`).
   - **Citations:** each cited source is one of §2.0, the cited chapter or page exists, and it really supports what the page uses it for. Each claim on the page is supported by a cited source or is standard and correct.
   - **Definitions and claims:** anything the page states differently from the source, or that no source supports. Wrong physics is an error even if a source says it.
   - **Only earlier terms:** list every word, symbol or idea the page uses before introducing it that is not in an earlier lesson (follow `prerequisites` and the block order in docs/HIGHSCHOOL-PLAN.md §4). A prerequisite that is planned but not written yet (the other PC may be writing it, docs/TWO-PC-WORKFLOW.md) counts as introduced if its lesson-list title and §4 row cover the idea; say so.
   - **Quiz and flashcards:** every marked answer is right, the other choices are wrong, and every explanation is true.
   - **Coverage:** the page covers what §4 lists for it, at VWO level; list topics it leaves out and say whether that matters at this level.
   - **"Where this leads" (`leadsTo`):** each target is a university lecture on this site (`{ course, lecture }`, e.g. `8.01`) on the same topic. The block's last lesson has `towards: true` and its claims about cosmology are correct.
   - **Licensing and AI terms:**
     - No sentence copied from a source (short standard phrases are fine; quote any suspect sentence beside the source's).
     - No figure copied from any source: every diagram is generated by the site.
     - Worked examples and quiz questions are not the source's own: compare with its examples and exercises.
     - No Siyavula logo, brand or "Everything Science" used as branding.
     - No MIT material marked "excluded from our Creative Commons license" or "All rights reserved".
     - NASA: no insignia, and no "according to NASA" phrasing.
     - ESO text used: the page's credits say AI helped rewrite it.
     - Nothing from a forbidden source (see the top of this file).
4. Write `reviews/<course>/<slug>.md` using the template below. Overwrite an older review.

## Practice sets (`kind: problems`)
A practice set is reviewed in two passes. There is no published solution to compare with, so the first pass is the check.
1. **Solve first, blind.** Read only the statements: the statement text of each `<Exercise>` and `<Part>`. Do not open the page's `<Hint>`, `<Solution>` or `<Answer>` blocks, and do not read the calcs file. (Whoever starts the review should hand over the statements with those blocks removed.) Solve every problem and part in full and write the final answers at the top of the review file before going on.
2. **Then compare.** Open the rest of the page and check:
   - each final answer against yours (an equivalent form counts as equal; check by value at a few points);
   - every step of each solution, and that it ends with a check (units, a limit, or a number);
   - each `<Answer>`: the `expr` or `value` is the solution's result, the `vars` ranges keep the formula finite and physical, and each `mistakes` hint is true of that wrong formula;
   - that every problem uses only the block's lessons, and that no problem is copied from a source's exercises;
   - that no hint gives the result away.
3. **Any disagreement between your answer and the page's is an error** until one side is shown wrong by a calculation (a calcs entry or a numerical check written into the review).
Add a section `## Blind solve` to the review (your answers, then the comparison, one line per answer id).

Severity is `error` (wrong physics, wrong number, wrong or unsupported citation, a term used before it is introduced, a licence or AI-terms problem, a forbidden source), `warning` (unclear, incomplete, or differs from the source without saying so) or `info`. Do not edit the page itself. Report only what you verified; mark anything uncertain as uncertain.
